What does a social media manager do?

The real scope of social media management, the posting rules of thumb people search for, how to choose platforms and tools, what a useful report looks like, and how to pick an agency.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 10 min read

A social media manager runs an organization's presence on social platforms from start to finish: they set the strategy, plan a content calendar, create or commission the posts, publish them, answer comments and messages, and report what worked. For many businesses that means two to four platforms, a steady weekly posting rhythm, fast replies (about three-quarters of consumers expect a brand to respond within 24 hours, per the 2025 Sprout Social Index), and a monthly report tied to business goals. A social media marketing agency does the same work with a team, often adding photo, video, design and paid ads.

What does social media management do?

Social media management turns a set of accounts into a working channel. Without it, accounts tend to post in bursts, go quiet for weeks and leave comments unanswered. With it, the organization shows up on a predictable schedule, sounds the same everywhere, and learns month by month what its audience responds to.

The work falls into six areas.

AreaWhat it covers
StrategyGoals, audiences, platforms, voice, posting frequency, what success means
Content planningA monthly calendar of themes, campaigns, dates and post formats
Content creationCaptions, graphics, photos, short-form video, Stories
PublishingScheduling each post in the right size and format for each platform
Community managementReplying to comments and messages, moderating, flagging issues
MeasurementMonthly reporting, testing and adjusting the plan

What does a social media manager do day to day?

A typical week for a social media manager looks something like this:

  • Monday. Review last week's results, confirm the week's calendar, and gather anything new from the team (an event, a product, a hire, a customer story).
  • Midweek. Write captions, brief or produce graphics and video, get approvals, and schedule posts.
  • Every day. Check comments, messages and mentions, reply to routine questions, and send anything sensitive to the right staff member.
  • As it happens. Capture events and behind-the-scenes moments for Stories and short video.
  • End of month. Build the report, compare results to goals, and plan next month.

The job uses several distinct skills: writing, design, photo and video, customer service, data analysis and project management. Few people are expert at all of them, which is one reason many organizations hire a team instead of a single person.

What is the 5-3-1 rule for social media?

The 5-3-1 rule is an informal engagement routine, not an industry standard. The most common version, described in a 2025 explainer from EngageDM, says that each time you publish a post, you like five posts from accounts in your niche or target audience, leave three comments that add something meaningful, and follow one new account that fits your brand or ideal customer.

The idea is that social platforms reward accounts that take part in conversations, not just broadcast. Versions of the rule differ from source to source. Treat it as a reminder to spend time engaging with others, not as a formula.

What is the 5-5-5 rule for social media?

The 5-5-5 rule is another informal daily engagement habit. In a June 2026 article, the National Association of Realtors described it as about 15 minutes a day spent on three five-minute tasks: commenting thoughtfully on five people's posts, replying to five Stories, and sending five direct messages to people in your network. The article credits Realtor.com trainer Angie Javier with recommending it in a webinar and notes the method is often cited by digital marketers and content creators.

Like the 5-3-1 rule, it is a habit people use to stay consistent, not a measured standard. Its value is in the routine: a few minutes of real conversation every day keeps an account active between posts.

What is the 50/30/20 rule for social media?

The 50/30/20 rule is a content mix guideline. As described by PR Daily (August 2026), about 50% of your posts are original content that informs, engages or entertains without selling, about 30% is curated content from trusted outside sources such as industry news or research, and about 20% is promotional. The author calls it a rule of thumb, not an ironclad rule: a big product launch might push promotion past 20% for a day, but the balance should hold steady over the year.

It is not the same as the 50/30/20 personal budgeting rule (needs, wants, savings), which often shows up in the same search results.

Other posting ratios you will see

Several similar ratios circulate in marketing writing. None is a formal standard; each is a way to keep a feed from becoming all advertising. Semrush describes three of the most common:

RuleWhat it says
5-3-2Of every 10 posts, 5 curated from others, 3 original, 2 personal or fun posts that humanize the brand
4-1-1Of every 6 posts, 4 curated, 1 original, 1 promotional
3-1For every 3 engagement posts, 1 promotional post
50/30/2050% original, 30% curated, 20% promotional (PR Daily)

Our view: the common thread matters more than the exact numbers. Most of what you post should give people a reason to follow you (useful information, real stories, good visuals), and a smaller share should ask for something. Organizations with strong original photo and video can lean further toward original content than the curated-heavy ratios suggest.

How to choose social media platforms

Start with where your audience already spends time. Pew Research Center's 2026 survey, published in October 2026, found that 84% of U.S. adults use YouTube and 70% use Facebook, with about half using Instagram (Pew Research Center). Age changes the picture sharply: 79% of adults 18 to 29 use Instagram and 65% use TikTok, while among adults 65 and older, 59% use Facebook and 11% use TikTok.

PlatformTypically strongest for
FacebookLocal businesses, community groups, events, adults 30 and up
InstagramVisual brands, younger adults, Reels and Stories
YouTubeLonger video, how-to content, search
TikTokShort-form video, reaching younger audiences
LinkedInB2B companies, recruiting, professional services

Two or three platforms done well usually serve an organization better than five done poorly. Pick the ones your customers use, commit to a schedule you can keep, and add platforms only when the first ones run smoothly.

Social media management tools

Most managers work from a scheduling and inbox tool so they can plan, post and reply from one place.

  • Meta Business Suite is Meta's free tool for scheduling and managing Facebook and Instagram posts and messages (Meta).
  • Buffer offers a free plan for up to three channels, with paid plans from $5 a month per channel and a 50% discount for nonprofits (Buffer pricing, 2026).
  • Hootsuite plans start at $99 a month, according to Hootsuite (June 2026).
  • Sprout Social is a premium tool aimed at teams, with per-seat pricing listed on its site.
  • Design and editing tools such as Canva, Adobe Creative Cloud and mobile video editors handle the content itself.

A tool does not create the content or decide what to say. It saves time on the mechanics so the manager can spend more time on strategy, creative and replies.

What a useful social media report includes

A good monthly report answers three questions: what happened, why, and what changes next month. It should include:

  • Reach and impressions by platform, compared to last month.
  • Engagement (reactions, comments, shares, saves) and engagement rate.
  • Follower growth, net of unfollows.
  • Clicks and conversions: website visits, sign-ups, calls, sales or donations traced to social.
  • Top and bottom posts, with a short note on why they performed that way.
  • Response time to comments and messages.
  • Next month's plan, including what will be tested.

Tie the report to business goals. A post with fewer likes that drove 40 booked appointments did more than a viral post that drove none.

What does a social media marketing agency do?

A social media marketing agency handles the full job described above with a team: typically a strategist or account lead, writers, designers, photo and video producers, a community manager and, often, a paid ads specialist. Agencies also bring process (approval workflows, brand guidelines, reporting templates) and continuity, so posting does not stop when one person is out.

Many agencies also run paid social ads, which is a separate discipline from organic posting. Ad management fees and ad spend are usually billed separately. For a breakdown of what agencies and freelancers charge, see our guide to social media management cost.

Social media manager for small business

A small business usually needs fewer platforms but the same fundamentals: a clear plan, good visuals, consistent posting and quick replies. A practical starting scope is one or two platforms where your customers already are, two to four quality posts a week, Stories or short video when something is happening, and same-day replies to messages.

A common gap is content. Owners often know what to say but do not have the photos and video to say it well. Building a library of professional photos of your team, space and work, plus a few short videos each month, gives any manager far more to work with.

How to choose a social media agency

  1. Look at accounts they manage now. Ask for two or three, and review the last 30 days of posts.
  2. Ask who does the work. Find out who writes, designs, films and answers comments on your account.
  3. Ask for a sample calendar. A month of planned posts shows how they think about your business.
  4. Ask for a sample report. It should explain results and next steps, not just list numbers.
  5. Check content capability. Confirm whether they produce original photo and video or rely on stock and templates.
  6. Confirm account ownership. Your organization should keep admin access to every account and own the content you paid for.
  7. Agree on approvals and response rules. Decide what posts need your sign-off and what questions they can answer directly.

Frequently asked questions

What does social media management do?

Social media management plans, creates, publishes and measures an organization's social media content and manages conversations with its audience. It covers strategy, a content calendar, content creation, scheduling, replying to comments and messages, and monthly reporting.

What is the 5-3-1 rule for social media?

The 5-3-1 rule is an informal engagement habit: for each post you publish, like five posts in your niche, leave three meaningful comments, and follow one relevant new account. It is a rule of thumb that marketers share, not a platform standard, and versions vary.

What is the 5 5 5 rule for social media?

The 5-5-5 rule is a daily engagement routine of about 15 minutes. As described by the National Association of Realtors in 2026, you comment on five people's posts, reply to five Stories, and send five direct messages to people in your network.

What is the 50/30/20 rule for social media?

The 50/30/20 rule suggests roughly 50% original content that informs or entertains, 30% curated content from trusted sources, and 20% promotional posts. It is a flexible guideline for keeping a feed from becoming all advertising.

What does a social media marketing agency do?

A social media marketing agency plans strategy, creates content, publishes posts, manages comments and messages, runs paid social ads and reports results, using a team of specialists. Many also produce original photo, video and graphics for the accounts they manage.

What are the different types of social media marketing?

The main types are organic content (regular posts, Stories and video), community management (replies and conversations), paid social advertising, influencer and partner marketing, and social listening (tracking what people say about your brand or topic). Most programs combine organic posting with community management, and add ads when there is a budget.

How can I start social media management for my business?

Choose one or two platforms your customers use, write down your goals and voice, build a monthly content calendar, and set a posting schedule you can keep. Use a scheduling tool, reply to comments and messages daily, and review results each month. Hiring a professional team gives you strategy and original content from the start.

Will AI replace social media marketing?

AI tools now help with drafting captions, resizing graphics and summarizing reports, but they do not replace knowing your audience, capturing real photos and video of your work, or judging what to say in a sensitive moment. Good tools work best in the hands of people who understand the brand.
