Nashville mayor election 2027: the date, the candidates, the runoff rules and what it takes to run

Everything specific to the 2027 race for Nashville mayor: the calendar, who has announced, how Metro's majority rule works, campaign finance limits and what past mayoral races cost and drew.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated November 2026 · 11 min read

The next Nashville mayoral election is Thursday, August 5, 2027. If no candidate wins a majority of the vote, the top two finishers meet in a runoff, which in 2019 and 2023 came six weeks after the August election. Mayor Freddie O'Connell, elected in 2023, is running for a second term, and Metro Councilmember Joy Styles, activist Lou Wilbanks and Natisha Brooks have also been reported as candidates. To make the ballot, a candidate needs a nominating petition signed by 25 registered Davidson County voters, filed by the qualifying deadline in May 2027.

This guide covers the mayor's race only. For the full 2027 Metro ballot, including vice mayor, Metro Council and the council-size case, see our Nashville 2027 Metro election guide.

When is the next Nashville mayoral election?

The general election is set for August 5, 2027, the date listed for Nashville's next election by the University of Tennessee's Municipal Technical Advisory Service. Metro holds its city elections in odd-numbered years, on the first Thursday in August.

That date held up through a challenge this year. A 2026 bill in the General Assembly would have moved municipal elections to the August or November state election dates, which Axios reported would have pushed Metro's election back a year and extended the current terms. The bill passed the House in April 2026 but died without becoming law, so the 2027 schedule stands.

The 2027 mayoral calendar

Some dates are set by statute. Others follow the pattern of past cycles and should be confirmed with the Davidson County Election Commission when it posts the official 2027 calendar.

The qualifying rule comes from T.C.A. § 2-5-101, which sets the deadline for municipal elections not held with the state's August general election at noon on the third Thursday of the third calendar month before the election. That formula produced May 18 in 2023, and it lands on May 20 in 2027. The 90-day petition window is in T.C.A. § 2-5-102. The Statement of Interests deadline and the early voting window come from the Election Commission's candidate page and early voting page.

Who is running for mayor of Nashville in 2027?

As of November 2026, these candidates have been reported by Nashville news outlets:

  • Freddie O'Connell (incumbent). He confirmed his re-election bid to FOX 17 on May 5, 2026 and held a campaign kickoff on July 13, 2026, at Slim and Husky's in North Nashville, according to the Nashville Scene.
  • Joy Styles. The District 32 councilmember, in her second term representing the Antioch area, announced her campaign on January 20, 2026, at the Southeast Community Center, as WPLN reported. She was the first challenger to declare.
  • Lou Wilbanks. A West Nashville activist, named as a candidate by the Nashville Scene and FOX 17.
  • Natisha Brooks. Named as a mayoral candidate by both outlets. The Scene notes she is also running as a Republican for Tennessee's 6th Congressional District. She ran for mayor in 2023 as well.

Announcing is not the same as qualifying. No one is officially on the ballot until the Election Commission certifies petitions after the May 2027 deadline, and Nashville fields tend to grow late. In 2023 the field was large enough that the eventual winner took just 27% in the first round.

Axios has also listed state Rep. John Ray Clemmons, a 2019 mayoral candidate, and Matt Wiltshire, a 2023 candidate, as people who could enter. Neither has announced.

How the runoff works

Metro elections require a majority to win outright. As NewsChannel 5 explained, a mayoral candidate needs more than 50% of the vote in August, and if no one clears that bar, the top two finishers go to a runoff. The Metro Charter's authors built the runoff in on purpose, on the view that a head-to-head final produces the strongest winner.

In practice, the runoff has become the normal path to the mayor's office. Each of the last three regular elections went to one:

  • 2015: general on August 6, runoff on September 10
  • 2019: general on August 1, runoff on September 12
  • 2023: general on August 3, runoff on September 14

Treat the mayor's race as two campaigns. The August race is about finishing in the top two, often with 25% to 35% of the vote. The runoff is a six-week sprint where eliminated candidates' voters are up for grabs and the money has to be raised again under fresh contribution limits. Our runoff guide covers how to plan for it before August.

The three most recent regular elections show how fragmented first rounds are and how small the electorate is.

Look at the 2015 first round: three candidates within about two points of each other, with roughly 800 votes separating first from second. In 2023, O'Connell won the August round with 27,503 votes out of 101,335 cast, then won the September runoff with 72,989 votes out of 114,317, per the Election Commission's results for August and September. A mayoral campaign that can identify and turn out about 30,000 committed voters in August has a strong claim to a runoff spot.

Is the Nashville mayor race partisan?

No. Metro races are nonpartisan, so no party label appears on the ballot. Candidates still have party affiliations and voters often know them: O'Connell is a Democrat, and his 2023 runoff opponent, Alice Rolli, is a Republican. The absence of a label puts more weight on name recognition, endorsements, neighborhood networks and direct voter contact than in a partisan race, where the label does much of the work.

What the mayor controls: powers and budget

Nashville's mayor runs a consolidated city-county government, so the office carries more authority than most big-city mayors. According to Metro's own summary, the mayor:

  • Writes the operating budget each fiscal year
  • Can veto any ordinance the Metro Council passes, with an override requiring a two-thirds council vote
  • Supervises all departments created by the Metro Charter
  • Appoints residents to the boards and commissions that regulate how the city runs
  • Serves a four-year term, with a limit of two terms

The budget is the core of the job. The council gave final approval on June 16, 2026, to a $3.8 billion operating budget for fiscal 2027. Property taxes, schools, housing and transit sit at the center of nearly every Nashville mayoral race, and FOX 17's candidate coverage shows the 2027 field is already debating property taxes.

Who can run for mayor of Nashville?

The Metro Charter sets the qualifications. A mayoral candidate must:

  1. Be at least 30 years old by the start of the term
  2. Have lived in Davidson County for at least three continuous years before the election
  3. Continue living in Davidson County while in office

To qualify, pick up a nominating petition from the Election Commission at 1281 Murfreesboro Pike, collect signatures and addresses from 25 registered voters eligible to vote in the race, and return it by the qualifying deadline. The Commission itself suggests gathering 50, because signatures with addresses that do not match the voter's registration get thrown out and petitions can be challenged. Serious campaigns collect far more than that and file early in the window, not on deadline day.

Campaign finance rules for the mayor's race

Metro candidates follow Tennessee's campaign finance law, and they file with the Davidson County Election Commission rather than the state Registry, per the Commission's campaign finance guidance.

Before you raise a dollar

You must file an Appointment of Political Treasurer form with the Election Commission before you receive contributions or spend money, other than incidental costs of deciding whether to run. That requirement applies to incumbents running again too.

Contribution limits

The Tennessee Registry of Election Finance adjusts limits every two years. The 2025 and 2026 limits for local candidates are:

SourceLimit per election
Individual$1,900
Single PAC$10,100
All PACs combined (excluding party-controlled PACs)$153,500

Under the same rules, a general election and a runoff count as separate elections, so a donor who gives the maximum in August can give again for September. The Registry will publish the figures for 2027 elections before the cycle, and campaigns should build their donor plans around those numbers once posted.

Reporting deadlines

Tennessee's candidate guidelines set the schedule:

  • Early supplemental reports by January 31 and July 15 in the years before the election, for candidates who appoint a treasurer before January 16 of the election year
  • Quarterly reports in the election year, due April 10, July 10 and October 10, with the fourth quarter due January 25
  • Pre-general report filed at least seven days before the election, covering activity through the tenth day before
  • Pre-runoff report on the same seven-day and ten-day schedule
  • Interim reports for any contribution of $1,000 or more received in the final ten days before an election

These reports are public, and Nashville reporters read them closely. Early filings tell donors, endorsers and the press who is viable, so the first reports matter as much as the last. For a walkthrough of filing, see our guide to campaign finance reporting for local candidates.

What does it cost to run for mayor of Nashville?

A competitive Nashville mayoral campaign is a seven-figure operation. These are market figures from past races, not a budget recommendation:

  • O'Connell's 2023 campaign raised nearly $2 million in total, and he had already brought in more than $440,000 in 2025 for 2027, plus $77,000 transferred from his 2023 account, according to the Nashville Scene.
  • In 2023, Matt Wiltshire had lent his campaign about $800,000 and Jim Gingrich, who dropped out before the election, about $2.5 million, per the Scene's fundraising report.
  • As of July 24, 2023, before the August general, O'Connell had $419,901 cash on hand to Alice Rolli's $164,106, which included a personal loan of $145,839, as WPLN reported.

The money pays for what a countywide race demands: broadcast and streaming TV, digital ads, mail to likely voters, a field program, polling, staff, and the video and photography that feed all of it. With limits of $1,900 per person per election, raising $1.5 million to $2 million means building a donor base in the thousands and starting early. A challenger who waits until the qualifying window to raise money is spotting the incumbent more than a year.

Frequently asked questions

When is the next Nashville mayoral race?

The next Nashville mayoral election is Thursday, August 5, 2027. If no candidate wins more than 50% of the vote, the top two advance to a runoff, which in 2019 and 2023 was held six weeks later. That would put a 2027 runoff around September 16.

Who is running for mayor of Nashville in 2027?

Mayor Freddie O'Connell is seeking a second term. Councilmember Joy Styles, activist Lou Wilbanks and Natisha Brooks have been reported as candidates by Nashville news outlets. The official field will not be set until the qualifying deadline in May 2027.

Is the Nashville mayor a Democrat or Republican?

Mayor Freddie O'Connell is a Democrat. Metro elections are nonpartisan, though, so party labels do not appear on the ballot for mayor or council.

How long is the Nashville mayor's term?

The mayor serves a four-year term and can serve a maximum of two terms. O'Connell took office in 2023, so 2027 is his first re-election campaign.

What were the Nashville mayor election 2023 results?

Freddie O'Connell led the August 3, 2023 general with 27.1% of the vote, followed by Alice Rolli at 20.2%. O'Connell then won the September 14 runoff with 63.9% to Rolli's 36.0%. Turnout was 20.5% in August and 23.0% in September.

How many signatures do you need to run for mayor of Nashville?

You need signatures from 25 registered voters who are eligible to vote in the race. The Election Commission recommends collecting at least 50, since signatures with mismatched addresses are rejected.

How much can you donate to a Nashville mayoral candidate?

Under Tennessee's 2025 and 2026 limits, an individual can give a local candidate $1,900 per election and a PAC can give $10,100. The general election and the runoff count as separate elections.

Why does Nashville have mayoral runoffs?

The Metro Charter requires a majority to win. Its authors wanted a head-to-head final between the top two candidates, and with large fields, every regular mayoral election since 2015 has gone to a runoff.
