Brand videos: what they are, the examples worth studying, and how to plan one for your company or yourself

The difference between a brand video and an ad, five verified examples that changed how brands use film, practical ideas, and a planning guide for company and personal brand videos.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 10 min read

A brand video is a short film that shows what an organization (or a person) stands for and why it exists, rather than selling one product or offer. Good brand videos earn attention through story: a belief, a person, a problem worth solving. Most run 60 seconds to three minutes for websites and events, with 15 to 60 second vertical cuts for social. The best-known brand video examples, from Apple's "1984" to Dove's "Real Beauty Sketches," are remembered decades later because they sold an idea about the brand, not a feature list.

What is a brand video?

A brand video communicates identity. Where a product video answers "what does this do?" and an ad answers "why buy now?", a brand video answers "who are you, and why should I care?"

That makes it the video you use everywhere: the homepage hero, the opening of a pitch meeting, the gala screen, the top of a careers page, the pinned post on LinkedIn. It sets the tone for every other piece of content you make.

Video typeQuestion it answersShelf life
Brand videoWho are you and why do you exist?Two to four years
Product or explainer videoWhat does this do and how does it work?Until the product changes
Ad or promoWhy should I act now?Weeks to months
TestimonialDoes it actually work for people like me?One to three years

Because a brand video lasts so long, it is the one place where cutting corners costs the most. Viewers will see it for years.

Brand video examples worth studying

Each of these is well documented and taught in marketing courses. They are large-budget productions, but the principles scale to any organization.

Apple, "1984"

Apple introduced the Macintosh with a commercial directed by Ridley Scott that aired nationally during Super Bowl XVIII on January 22, 1984. It barely shows the product. Instead it casts Apple as the rebel breaking conformity. The lesson: a brand video can sell a worldview, and the product becomes proof of that worldview.

Dove, "Real Beauty Sketches"

In 2013, Dove filmed a forensic sketch artist drawing women first from their own descriptions, then from strangers' descriptions. The gap between the two portraits carried the whole message: women are more beautiful than they believe. The film won the Titanium Grand Prix at Cannes Lions, the festival's top honor that year. The lesson: a simple, real experiment with real people beats any claim a brand can make about itself.

Chipotle, "Back to the Start"

Chipotle's animated short, set to Willie Nelson's cover of Coldplay's "The Scientist," follows a farmer who moves away from industrial farming and returns to sustainable practices. It won the Film Grand Prix at Cannes Lions in 2012. The lesson: a brand video can tell the company's values without a spokesperson or a single line of dialogue, if the story is clear.

Nike, "Dream Crazy"

Narrated by Colin Kaepernick for the 30th anniversary of "Just Do It," the two-minute film featured athletes who overcame long odds. It won the 2019 Emmy for Outstanding Commercial. The lesson: a brand that takes a clear position will lose some people and bind others more tightly. Nike knew which audience it was speaking to.

Dollar Shave Club, "Our Blades Are F***ing Great"

Founder Michael Dubin starred in his company's 2012 launch video, which was shot in a day for less than $4,500. Unilever bought the company in 2016 in a deal reported at $1 billion. The lesson: the founder is often the most powerful asset a young brand has. It was a tight, sharply written script, delivered by someone who believed it, with careful comic timing. That craft is why it worked.

What every great brand video has in common

Across those examples and the work we see succeed for organizations of every size, the same elements show up.

  • One idea. "Women underestimate their own beauty." "Real food comes from real farms." If you cannot say the idea in one sentence, the video will not land.
  • A human at the center. A founder, a customer, an employee, a student, a farmer. People connect with people, not logos.
  • Show, do not claim. Process, evidence and real moments beat adjectives. Never say "innovative" when you can show the thing being built.
  • A distinct look and sound. Color, music, pacing and framing that match the brand and stay consistent across cutdowns.
  • An ending that tells people what to feel or do. Visit, apply, give, join, follow.

Brand video ideas

If you are stuck on a concept, start with one of these angles and adapt it.

  • The origin story. Why the organization started, told by the founder on location where it began.
  • The mission in action. Follow the work for a day: the service delivered, the student taught, the patient helped.
  • The customer's world. Center the people you serve. Your brand is the supporting character.
  • The craft. Slow, detailed footage of how your product or service is made. It works especially well for makers, restaurants, clothing brands and trades.
  • The manifesto. A written statement of belief read in voiceover over strong imagery. Requires excellent writing.
  • The team. Short portraits of the people who make the work happen, cut together into one film.
  • The milestone. An anniversary or expansion as the frame for telling the whole story.
  • The community. For churches, schools, nonprofits and local businesses, the neighborhood itself can be the main character.

For clothing brand videos and other product-driven brands, combine the craft angle with the community angle: how it is made, and who wears it.

Personal brand videos for founders and executives

A personal brand video does for a person what a brand video does for a company. Founders, executives, consultants, authors, speakers, pastors and candidates use them to introduce themselves to audiences they have not met yet: investors, boards, conference organizers, media bookers, voters and clients.

What a strong personal brand video includes:

  1. A clear point of view. Not a résumé read aloud. What do you believe about your field that others do not?
  2. Proof. Footage of you doing the work: speaking, leading a meeting, on a job site, with the people you serve.
  3. A natural on-camera interview. Shot with professional light and sound and directed, so you sound like yourself on a good day.
  4. Short versions. A 60 to 90 second main film, a 30-second version and several vertical clips of your best lines.
  5. Matching stills. Shoot your professional headshots on the same day so the video, the website and the speaker bio share one look.

Executives often worry they are not good on camera. Almost nobody is at first. A director who conducts the interview as a conversation, lets you restart freely and builds the story in the edit is the difference between a stiff recording and a film people share.

How to plan a brand video

  1. Write the one-sentence idea. Test it with people outside the organization.
  2. Pick the audience. Prospects, recruits, donors, partners. You can serve more than one, but rank them.
  3. Choose the story structure. Origin, mission in action, customer world, manifesto or team.
  4. Cast the people. Real staff, customers and leaders who can speak with feeling. Pre-interview them by phone.
  5. Scout and plan locations. Places that show the work, not just a nice backdrop.
  6. Decide the look. Reference films, color palette, music style and pacing, agreed before the shoot.
  7. Plan every deliverable. Main film, short cuts, vertical cuts, captioned versions and stills.
  8. Shoot with a professional crew. Interviews with controlled light and sound, then b-roll that proves every point.
  9. Edit for emotion first, information second. Then add music, graphics, captions and color.
  10. Launch it on purpose. A homepage update, an email to your list, native posts on each platform, and a screening at your next event.

These lengths are working ranges from production practice. Platforms keep raising their limits (Instagram expanded Reels uploads to three minutes in January 2025, and YouTube Shorts runs up to three minutes too), but allowed length is not the same as ideal length. Hook the viewer in the first two seconds, and design for sound off with captions.

Mistakes that sink brand videos

  • Trying to say everything. Ten messages means zero messages.
  • Leading with the logo. Open on a person or a moment, not an animated logo sting.
  • Stock footage as the story. Viewers recognize it, and it says nothing about you.
  • Weak audio. A beautiful image with hollow sound reads as amateur instantly.
  • No plan for cutdowns. If vertical versions are not planned at the shoot, they will be awkward crops later.
  • Templates and generators. A brand video's whole purpose is to show what is distinct about you. A template, by definition, cannot.

Frequently asked questions

What is a brand video?

A brand video is a short film that shows what an organization or person stands for and why it exists, rather than selling a specific product. It usually runs 60 seconds to three minutes and lives on the homepage, at events and across social channels. It sets the tone for every other piece of content you make.

What are some good brand video examples?

Well-documented examples include Apple's "1984," which introduced the Macintosh during Super Bowl XVIII, Dove's "Real Beauty Sketches," which won the Titanium Grand Prix at Cannes Lions in 2013, Chipotle's "Back to the Start," winner of the 2012 Cannes Film Grand Prix, and Nike's "Dream Crazy," which won the 2019 Emmy for Outstanding Commercial. Each sells an idea about the brand rather than a product feature.

What are some brand video ideas?

Strong starting points include an origin story told by the founder, the mission in action over one day, a film centered on the people you serve, a close look at your craft or process, a written manifesto in voiceover, and short team portraits. Pick one idea and one audience per video.

What is a personal brand video?

A personal brand video introduces a founder, executive, speaker, consultant or candidate to people who have not met them yet. It combines an on-camera interview with footage of the person doing their work, and it is usually delivered as a 60 to 90 second film plus short vertical clips. It works best when shot on the same day as matching headshots.

How long should a brand video be?

For a website or event screen, 60 seconds to three minutes. For social platforms, cut 15 to 60 second vertical versions, and for paid ads, 6 to 30 seconds. The first two seconds matter most on every platform.

What makes the best brand video?

The best brand videos have one clear idea, a real person at the center, footage that shows rather than claims, a distinct look and sound, and an ending that tells viewers what to do next. Professional sound, light and editing are what make those elements land.

How much does a brand video cost?

Professional corporate and brand videos generally run $1,500 to $20,000 per video in 2026, according to Vidico's market data, with premium productions much higher. Planning several deliverables from one shoot (main film, cutdowns, testimonials and stills) gets the most from the budget.
