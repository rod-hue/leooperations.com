The First Look at a Wedding: What It Is, Pros and Cons, and How to Plan One

A practical guide to deciding on a first look, setting it up well, and what it changes about your photos, video and timeline.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

A first look is a planned, private moment before the ceremony when a couple sees each other dressed for the first time, usually with only the photographer and videographer present. It moves most portraits before the ceremony, so you can join cocktail hour instead of spending it on photos. The Knot's 2023 Real Weddings Study found 56% of couples chose one. It is a good choice for most couples, but not for everyone, and the reasons on both sides are real.

What is a first look at a wedding?

A first look is a staged reveal. One partner waits, usually facing away, in a quiet spot chosen with the photographer. The other walks up, taps their shoulder, and they turn around. The photographer and videographer capture the walk, the turn and the reactions, then step back and give the couple a few minutes alone.

It started as a practical answer to a timeline problem. In a traditional schedule, the couple does not see each other until the aisle, so every portrait has to happen after the ceremony. A first look moves those photos earlier and gives the couple time with each other before the day gets loud.

First looks are not only for couples. Many families now plan first looks with a parent, a grandparent or the wedding party, and some couples add private vows or letters.

Pros and cons of a first look

Is it better to do a first look or not? Our honest opinion: if your ceremony is late in the afternoon, sunset is early, your family list is long, or you would hate missing cocktail hour, do a first look. If the aisle moment is the single image you have pictured for years, or your tradition holds that you do not see each other before the ceremony, skip it and plan your timeline around that choice. Both work. What does not work is skipping the first look and also wanting full cocktail hour and long portraits.

The Knot's own pros and cons of a first look makes many of the same points.

First look with dad, parents or the bridal party

These have become some of the most emotional moments of the day, and they are easy to add.

First look with dad or a parent

A parent first look usually happens right after getting dressed and before the couple's first look. Dad (or mom, a stepparent, a grandparent or a guardian) waits facing away, the partner walks up, and the photographer captures the reaction. Plan 10 minutes. It works best in a quiet room or hallway rather than in front of the whole wedding party.

This is especially meaningful if a parent will not walk you down the aisle, or if a grandparent cannot stand for long during the ceremony.

First look with the bridal party

The wedding party faces away, the partner walks in, and everyone turns at once. Reactions are usually loud and funny. Plan 10 minutes, and do it after the parent first look so the quieter moment is not overwhelmed.

Who does the bride do a first look with? Whoever matters most to her. Most often the partner, then a parent, then the wedding party, but grandparents, siblings and children from a previous relationship all work beautifully.

First touch instead of first look

If you want the photos without seeing each other, a first touch (holding hands around a door or corner) or exchanging letters back to back keeps the aisle reveal intact. Videographers love these because the audio of each partner reading is intimate and clear.

Private vows during a first look

Some couples use the first look to read private vows or letters, then keep the ceremony vows traditional. It gives you space to say something personal without an audience, and it gives the video team an unusually clean recording.

Tips that help:

  • Write your vows on cards, not your phone.
  • Tell the videographer in advance so they mic both of you.
  • Keep it to one or two minutes each.
  • Pick a spot away from guest arrival paths and loud air conditioning.

Can you kiss during a first look? Yes. There is no rule against it. Most couples hug first and kiss after, and photographers usually step back to give you that moment. If you want to save your first kiss as a married couple for the ceremony, just tell your photographer in advance.

How a first look changes your timeline

A first look shifts about 90 minutes of portraits from after the ceremony to before it. Here is the difference in a typical 4:00 pm ceremony:

Part of dayWith a first lookWithout a first look
Couple portraits2:15 pm, before ceremony5:20 pm, after family and wedding party
Wedding party photos2:45 pm5:00 pm
Immediate family3:15 pm4:30 pm
Couple at cocktail hourYes, most of itUsually misses most of it
Photographer arrivalEarlierLater
Hair and makeup finishAbout 1:30 pmAbout 2:30 pm

The trade-off is a slightly earlier start. Hair and makeup has to finish about an hour sooner, and coverage often needs to begin earlier. The full sample schedules for 4, 6 and 8 hours of coverage are in our wedding photography timeline.

A few timing details we plan for:

  • Finish the first look at least 30 minutes before guests arrive, so nobody sees the couple.
  • Plan 10 minutes per parent or bridal party first look.
  • Keep 10 to 15 minutes of buffer after the first look. Makeup touch-ups after tears are real.
  • Do immediate family photos before the ceremony if possible, and catch extended family after it.

How photographers and videographers capture a first look

A good first look is planned like a small scene.

  • Location. Shaded, quiet and private, with a clean background. Open shade beats harsh direct sun. Indoors, a big window works well.
  • Positions. The photographer usually stands where they can see the waiting partner's face when they turn. A second photographer or videographer captures the walk from behind and a wide angle.
  • Audio. Videographers mic at least one partner so the words are recorded. If you are reading letters, both partners should be mic'd.
  • Direction. The photographer cues the walk, then gets quiet. The best first looks are not posed after the turn.
  • Time alone. After a few minutes of reactions, the photographers step away to give the couple privacy.

With two camera professionals, one can stay on faces while the other works wide and from behind, which is how you end up with both reactions in the gallery.

Unplugged ceremony tips

An unplugged ceremony asks guests to put phones and cameras away during the ceremony. Requests are growing: The Knot reported a 17% increase in unplug requests over five years, according to Spectrum News coverage from June 2024.

Why it matters for photos and video: phones held up in the aisle show up in the processional shots, and tablets block the videographer's angle on the vows. A guest stepping into the aisle at the kiss can ruin the one frame you wanted most.

How to do it well:

  • Put a short line on the wedding website and a sign at the ceremony entrance.
  • Have the officiant make a friendly announcement right before the processional.
  • Promise guests you will share professional photos, and tell them when.
  • Consider a "plugged" moment after the recessional, when everyone can take their own photos.
  • Ask ushers to gently remind guests who forget.

A first look and an unplugged ceremony pair well. Your first sighting is already documented privately, and your aisle photos come out clean.

Frequently asked questions

What is a first look wedding?

A first look is a planned moment before the ceremony when the couple sees each other dressed for the first time, usually privately with only the photographer and videographer present. It lets most portraits happen before the ceremony so the couple can enjoy cocktail hour.

Can you kiss during a first look?

Yes, you can kiss during a first look. Most couples hug and then kiss, and the photographer usually steps back to give them a moment. If you want to save your first kiss for the ceremony, tell your photographer in advance so they do not cue you.

Who does the bride do a first look with?

Most often with her partner, but many brides also do first looks with their dad or mom, grandparents, siblings or the bridal party. Plan about 10 minutes for each extra first look and do the quieter, family ones before the bridal party.

Is it better to do a first look or not?

It depends on your priorities. A first look gives you more time with guests and better light for portraits, especially in winter. Skip it if seeing each other at the aisle is the moment you care about most or your tradition asks you to wait.

Does a first look ruin the aisle moment?

Usually not. Many couples still get emotional at the ceremony because the setting, the music and the guests are different. The aisle reaction may be calmer, which some couples see as a plus.

How long does a first look take?

The reveal itself takes a few minutes, but plan about 15 minutes for the first look plus a few minutes alone. Add 10 minutes for each parent or wedding party first look, and a short buffer for makeup touch-ups.

What is an unplugged wedding ceremony?

An unplugged ceremony asks guests to put away phones and cameras until after the recessional. It keeps devices out of your professional photos and video, and lets guests actually watch the ceremony.
