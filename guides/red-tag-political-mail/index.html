Red tag political mail: what USPS Tag 57 does and how campaigns use it to land mail on time

Who can red tag a mailing, what the tag changes inside the Postal Service, what it does not change, and how to schedule drops so every piece lands before voters cast their ballots.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated November 2026 · 11 min read

Red tag political mail is campaign or advocacy mail whose trays, sacks and pallets carry USPS Tag 57, a red container tag printed with the words "Political Campaign Mailing." The tag does not buy a faster mail class. It flags the container as Political Mail. Postal facilities are told to document that mail as Political Mail and to process and deliver it promptly and efficiently. Political Mail still goes as ordinary First-Class Mail or USPS Marketing Mail. The Postal Service still asks mailers to allow roughly 3 to 10 business days for Marketing Mail and 2 to 5 for First-Class before reporting a delivery delay.

That last point is where campaigns get burned. A red tag makes your mail visible and makes someone accountable for it. It cannot rescue a Marketing Mail drop that reached the post office four days before Election Day.

What is red tag political mail?

"Red tag" is the industry shorthand for mail identified with USPS Tag 57. The Postal Service defines Political Mail as material mailed for campaign purposes by a registered candidate, campaign committee or party committee. It also covers material mailed by a political action committee or by an organization doing issue advocacy or voter mobilization. It can be sent for any public election, partisan or nonpartisan, for which there is a ballot.

USPS splits that into two groups, and both use the same red tag:

  • Political Campaign Mail: sent by a registered candidate, a federal, state or local campaign committee, or a party committee to promote candidates, referendums or campaigns (Postal Bulletin).
  • Political Message Mail: sent by a PAC, a super PAC, or another organization engaged in campaign activity, issue advocacy or voter mobilization (USPS News Link).

The tag is red, with "Political Campaign Mailing" printed in black and six black stars above and below the words (USPS FAQ). It goes on the container, not the mailpiece, so voters never see it.

Who can use red Tag 57, and who cannot

Any registered candidate, campaign committee or party committee can red tag its mail. So can PACs, super PACs and organizations running issue or voter mobilization mail (Postal Bulletin). USPS defines a registered candidate or party as one recognized by a government election control authority at the national, state or local level. That covers a school board candidate as much as a Senate campaign. If you run independent expenditures, our guide to super PACs and independent expenditures covers the spending side.

Election offices are a different category. In October 2026 USPS reminded employees that only Political Mail can use Red Tag 57, while only Ballot Mail may use Green Tag 191.

What the red tag does inside the Postal Service

The tag triggers process. Each election cycle, USPS directs its facilities to:

  • use Tag 57 correctly on all Political Mail
  • tell employees they must promptly and efficiently process and deliver it
  • document as Political Mail all qualifying mail entered into the mailstream

In practice that means:

  • A paper trail. If employees find Political Mail that arrived without a tag, they are told to enter it in the appropriate log. Then they report it to the local Election Mail or Political Mail coordinator (USPS News).
  • All-clear checks. Facilities complete a daily all-clear certification for Election and Political Mail. After the 2026 primaries, the Inspector General recommended adding a check that the Late Arriving Political Mail Report was completed to that daily certification.
  • Named people. USPS publishes area Election Mail and Political Mail coordinators, so a campaign problem has somewhere to go.

The track record is strong when the system is used. The USPS Office of Inspector General looked at December 1, 2023, to April 30, 2024. In that period the Postal Service processed Political and Election Mail with on-time processing scores of 97.01 to 98.17 percent.

The failures are instructive too. For the 2026 primaries, the OIG observed nine processing facilities and 73 delivery units in eight states from March to May. Its September 2026 report counted 214 delayed pieces, 77 of them Political Mail. It issued 11 recommendations. One asked USPS to pause scheduled operational changes, including regional transportation optimization, during the 2026 midterm season.

Our read: to a clerk on an overnight shift, an untagged tray of campaign postcards looks like every other tray of Marketing Mail. The tag makes it something a supervisor has to account for. Red tag every container, from the introduction card to the last GOTV reminder.

The red tag is one of three identifiers, plus an advance notice

USPS now describes Political Mail as identified three ways: red Tag 57, the Political Campaign Mailing checkbox at induction, and Political Mail STIDs. A well-run drop uses all three. It adds a fourth step before the mail even arrives.

The Political Campaign Mailing checkbox

On top of the tag, mailers must check the "Political Campaign Mailing" box when they present Political Mail to USPS for processing. Your mail house usually prepares the postage statement, so ask them to confirm the box is checked on every drop.

Political Mail Service Type IDs

A Service Type ID (STID) is a three-digit code inside the Intelligent Mail barcode. It tells USPS the service type, mail class and any address correction service for each piece. USPS offers STIDs built specifically for Political Mail. It says they help it identify and track Political Mail and raise its visibility in automation. The current STID table lives on PostalPro.

The Political Mailing Notice

USPS runs an online Political Mailing Notice that lets a campaign alert the Postal Service before a drop. You provide a copy of the mailpiece and the PS Form 3600 or 3602. USPS says this helps it plan for receiving your mail in advance. File one for every drop.

Marketing Mail or First-Class for political mail?

Red tagging works with either class, so the class decision is about budget and calendar. The table uses USPS prices for letter-size automation pieces in effect since July 12, 2026 (Notice 123). Flat-size pieces such as full-page mailers cost more in both classes.

Take 20,000 letter-size pieces sorted to 5-digit. Marketing Mail postage comes to about $7,900. First-Class comes to about $12,420. That is a $4,520 difference for one drop.

The July 2026 table puts Nonprofit Marketing Mail as low as $0.185 per 5-digit automation letter. Only a short list of political committees can use those prices: a party's national and state committees and the congressional and senatorial campaign committees named in DMM 703.1.3.1. A candidate's own committee is not on that list. Even a listed committee needs USPS authorization before it mails at nonprofit prices.

Our rule of thumb: send persuasion mail as Marketing Mail. Pay for First-Class wherever a three-day slip would cost you votes. That means the last piece before early voting closes and chase mail to voters holding an unreturned mail ballot. It also means anything in a runoff with a short calendar. For the full program math, see political direct mail and texting and how to build a political campaign budget.

How campaigns use red tag mail: timing the drops

Plan from in-home dates (the day the piece should be in mailboxes) backward to drop dates. A common mistake is setting the last drop date too late. The campaign thinks about Election Day instead of the day its voters actually vote. With early voting and mail ballots, a large share of your targets may have voted before the final weekend.

The planning windows below are our recommendations, not a USPS rule. They assume red tagged, presorted mail with the checkbox, Political Mail STIDs and an advance notice.

Two more habits separate campaigns that land mail on time from campaigns that blame the post office:

  1. Attach the print schedule to the mail calendar. Proof approval, printing, addressing and presort often take longer than the postal leg.
  2. Coordinate mail with doors and digital. A voter who gets your contrast piece on Tuesday should meet a canvasser with the same message on Thursday. Our get-out-the-vote plan guide and canvassing guide cover the field side of that schedule.

How to send red tag political mail, step by step

  1. Confirm eligibility. Political Campaign Mail and Political Message Mail both qualify for Tag 57. Get the paid-for-by disclaimer right under FEC and state rules before anything prints.
  2. Pick the class for each piece. Use the table above and your calendar.
  3. Design for automation. Letter-size pieces that meet automation standards get the best prices. Make sure the Intelligent Mail barcode carries a Political Mail STID.
  4. File the Political Mailing Notice. Provide the mailpiece and PS Form 3600 or 3602 at tools.usps.com before the drop.
  5. Tag every container. Trays, sacks and pallets all get Tag 57. Tags are available at Business Mail Entry Units.
  6. Check the box. The Political Campaign Mailing checkbox goes on the postage statement when you present the mail.
  7. Seed the list and watch the scans. Put staff and volunteer addresses across the district on every list. Record when each piece arrives and compare it to the barcode scan data your mail house can pull.
  8. Keep the paperwork. Save the postage statement, drop time, entry facility and tag photos for every drop.

What to do if red tag political mail is late

Give the piece the full service window first. USPS asks mailers to allow about 3 to 10 business days for Marketing Mail and 2 to 5 for First-Class before reporting a delay (USPS FAQ). After that, report the problem through the online help form linked from the USPS political mail page. Have a copy of the mailpiece and the PS Form 3600 or 3602 ready.

The 2026 OIG report recommended that USPS define resolution time milestones for Election and Political Mail concerns submitted through its Election Mail website (USPS OIG). So document everything. Seed list arrival dates, scan data and tag photos turn a complaint into a case a coordinator can act on.

Frequently asked questions

What is USPS red tag political mail?

It is Political Mail entered with USPS Tag 57, a red container tag reading "Political Campaign Mailing." The tag identifies trays, sacks and pallets of campaign and advocacy mail so postal facilities document them and process them promptly. It applies to any public election with a ballot, partisan or nonpartisan.

Does red tag political mail cost extra?

No. Political Mail is mailed at regular First-Class Mail or USPS Marketing Mail prices (Postal Bulletin). You pay three things:

- the postage for your class

- the $390 annual mailing fee for that class

- your printer and mail house charges

The tags come from Business Mail Entry Units.

Is Tag 57 required on political mail?

USPS describes Tag 57 as a tag that may be used to identify Political Mail. It also directs its facilities to make sure the tag is used correctly on all Political Mail. The Political Campaign Mailing checkbox is stated as a requirement: mailers must check it. Treat both as mandatory on every drop.

How long does red tag political mail take to deliver?

The tag does not change your class's delivery window. USPS asks mailers to allow about 3 to 10 business days for Marketing Mail and 2 to 5 for First-Class before reporting a delay (USPS FAQ). Plan drop dates with those windows in mind.

Can a PAC, super PAC or nonprofit use red tag mail?

Yes. Political Message Mail from a PAC, super PAC or organization doing issue advocacy or voter mobilization qualifies for Tag 57 (Postal Bulletin). Nonprofit postage prices are a separate question with separate eligibility rules under DMM 703.

What is the difference between the red tag and the green tag?

Red Tag 57 is for Political Mail from campaigns, parties and political organizations. Green Tag 191 is for Ballot Mail, and USPS says no other form of Election Mail can use it. A campaign never uses the green tag.

Can I track red tag political mail?

Yes, if your pieces carry a serialized Intelligent Mail barcode with a Political Mail STID in it. USPS uses that barcode to sort and track letters and flats. It says Political Mail STIDs help it identify and track Political Mail in the mailstream (Postal Bulletin).
