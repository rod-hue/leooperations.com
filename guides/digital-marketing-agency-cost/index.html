What Does a Digital Marketing Agency Do, and How Much Does It Cost?

The services a digital marketing agency provides, 2026 market prices for retainers, hourly and project work, and how to choose a partner you can trust.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 11 min read

A digital marketing agency plans and runs the online work that brings customers to a business: the website, search engine optimization (SEO), social media, paid ads, email and text messaging, content and the reporting that ties it together. In 2026, Clutch places U.S. agencies in its $100 to $149 an hour band, and most projects reviewed on the platform fall between $10,000 and $49,999. Monthly retainers for ongoing work commonly run from about $1,000 for a single service to $20,000 or more for a full program, per WebFX's 2026 pricing guide. These are market ranges, not Leo Operations prices, and ad spend is billed separately.

What does a digital marketing agency do?

An agency does three jobs at once. It decides where your marketing money should go, it produces the work (pages, posts, ads, emails, videos), and it measures what happened so the next month is better than the last.

For a small business, that usually means a team of specialists that would cost far more to hire one by one: a strategist, a designer, a copywriter, a paid ads manager, an SEO specialist and someone who reads the analytics. The median pay for web developers and digital designers alone was $99,520 a year in May 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. An agency spreads the cost of that skill across many clients, so each one pays for the hours it uses.

A good agency also keeps your channels speaking with one voice. The ad, the landing page, the follow-up email and the Instagram post should look and sound like the same business. When five different vendors handle five channels, keeping that consistency takes more work.

Here are the core services, in plain terms. Most agencies offer several of them; full-service agencies offer all of them.

Website

Every other channel sends people to your website, so it is usually the first thing an agency checks. Slow pages, broken forms and a site that is hard to use on a phone waste ad money. In September 2026, 49.1% of U.S. web traffic came from mobile devices, slightly more than desktop, per StatCounter. Our website design cost guide covers what a site should include and what it costs.

SEO

SEO is the slow, compounding work of earning free search traffic. It includes technical fixes, writing pages that answer the questions customers actually type, and building your local presence on Google Maps. It takes months to show results, which is why most SEO is sold monthly.

Social media management

This is the content calendar, the design and video, the posting and the conversations in comments and messages. For a detailed breakdown, see our guides on social media management cost and what a social media manager does.

Paid ads

Paid search and paid social put your business in front of people right away. The agency's job is to target the right people, write and design the ads, set up conversion tracking and cut spending on what is not working. Ad spend goes to Google or Meta; the agency charges a separate management fee.

Email and SMS

Email and text reach people who already know you: past customers, members, donors or subscribers. An agency sets up the list, the templates and automated messages such as a welcome series or appointment reminder, and keeps your sending within the rules for consent and opt-outs.

Content

Content is the raw material for everything above: photos, short videos, graphics, articles and case studies. Original photos and video show customers your actual people and place. See our video production cost guide for what professional video adds to a budget.

Agencies price their work in three ways: monthly retainers, hourly billing and fixed-price projects. Many combine them, for example a one-time website project followed by a monthly retainer for SEO and social.

How much should a business budget for marketing overall?

Agency fees are one part of a marketing budget. In Gartner's 2025 CMO Spend Survey, marketing budgets averaged 7.7% of company revenue, with half of chief marketing officers reporting 6% or less. That survey covered 402 senior marketing leaders in North America, the U.K. and Europe, so treat it as a reference point rather than a rule for a small business. The practical approach is to start from a goal (calls, bookings, sales, sign-ups), estimate what a new customer is worth to you, and set the budget from there.

Digital marketing services for small business: where to start

A small business rarely needs every service on day one. The order matters more than the number of channels.

  1. Fix the foundation. A working website, a complete Google Business Profile and conversion tracking. Without tracking, nobody can tell you which dollars worked.
  2. Show up where people are already searching. Local SEO and, if you need leads quickly, a focused paid search campaign on your most profitable service.
  3. Stay visible to people who know you. Consistent social posting and a simple monthly email.
  4. Add content that builds trust. Real photos of your team and work, short videos and customer stories.
  5. Expand paid social and remarketing once the site converts and tracking is in place.

A focused program on two or three channels, done well every month, usually does more than a thin presence on seven.

How to choose a digital marketing agency

Ask to see work for businesses like yours, and ask what happened after it launched. Then ask these questions:

  • Who will actually do the work? Meet the people on your account, not only the salesperson.
  • What exactly is included each month? Number of posts, ads, pages, emails and meetings, in writing.
  • How will you measure success? The answer should name business results (calls, bookings, sales, donations), not only likes or impressions.
  • Who owns the accounts? Your website, domain, ad accounts, social pages, analytics and email list should be in your name, with the agency given access.
  • How do you report? A monthly report in plain language, with what worked, what did not and what changes next.
  • What is the contract term and how do I leave? Know the notice period and what you keep if you part ways.

Google's own advice on hiring an SEO is a good model for any agency: ask for examples of previous work, ask what results to expect and on what timeline, and ask whether they will document the changes they make and explain why.

Red flags when hiring an agency

  • Guaranteed rankings. Google states plainly that no one can guarantee a #1 ranking and warns against anyone who claims a special relationship with Google.
  • Cold emails promising page one. Google specifically advises caution with firms that email you out of the blue.
  • Secrecy about methods. If an agency will not explain what it plans to do, treat that as a warning sign.
  • Bought followers, likes or reviews. The FTC's rule on reviews and testimonials, announced in August 2024, bans fake reviews and prohibits buying or selling fake indicators of social media influence, such as bot followers or views, when used to misrepresent influence for a commercial purpose.
  • The agency owns your accounts. If the agency registers your domain, ad account or social pages in its own name, leaving becomes expensive.
  • Reports full of vanity metrics. Impressions and follower counts have their place, but a report should connect activity to inquiries and revenue.
  • No clear scope. "We will handle your social" is not a scope. A list of deliverables is.

Frequently asked questions

What does a digital marketing agency do?

A digital marketing agency plans, produces and measures a business's online marketing. That includes the website, SEO, social media, paid ads, email and SMS, content creation and analytics. The agency's value is a team of specialists working from one plan, so every channel supports the same goal.

How much does it cost to hire a digital marketing agency?

In 2026, Clutch places U.S. agencies in its $100 to $149 an hour band, and most projects reviewed there range from $10,000 to $49,999, per Clutch. Monthly retainers range from about $1,000 to $20,000 or more depending on scope, per WebFX. Ad spend is billed separately.

What does a digital marketing agency provide?

Most agencies provide strategy, website design, SEO, social media management, paid advertising, email and SMS marketing, content creation (photo, video, graphics and writing) and reporting. Full-service agencies provide all of these under one plan; specialists focus on one or two.

What are the services in digital marketing?

The main digital marketing services are website design and development, search engine optimization, social media marketing, pay-per-click and paid social advertising, email and SMS marketing, content marketing and analytics. Many agencies also offer video production, graphic design and reputation management.

How much does a freelance digital marketer charge?

Freelance rates vary widely by skill and location. Thumbtack's 2025 website development price guide puts an SEO consultant at about $50 to $75 an hour, while U.S. agencies on Clutch bill $100 to $149 an hour. The agency rate usually covers a team, project management and software that a single freelancer does not provide.

What is a typical PPC management fee?

Agencies commonly charge 10% to 20% of monthly ad spend or a flat fee of about $1,000 to $3,000 a month, according to WebFX's 2026 PPC pricing guide. The percentage usually drops as spend increases, and some agencies require a minimum monthly ad spend.

Is a digital marketing agency worth it for a small business?

An agency is worth it when the work is tied to a clear business goal and measured honestly. A small business gets a strategist, designer, ad manager and analyst for the cost of a monthly retainer, and the owner gets time back to run the business. Ask for reporting that connects the work to calls, bookings or sales.

How long does digital marketing take to work?

Paid ads can produce traffic and inquiries within days of launch. SEO and organic social build over months, because search rankings and audiences grow with consistent work. Plan on at least a few months of steady effort before judging the long-term channels.
