Planning Photo, Video and Event Media at Music City Center

A working planner's guide to the halls, ballrooms and terraces, the building's exclusive services, signage and badge rules, and the deadlines your coverage, livestream and print depend on.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated November 2026 · 13 min read

Music City Center lets you bring your own photographer, videographer and design team, but several services around them are exclusive to the building: electrical, internet and telecom, rigging, food and beverage, and move-in and move-out security, according to the MCC Event Planning Guide. Audio-visual is not exclusive (LMG is the preferred in-house partner), so your media team can work alongside your AV company. The practical result: order hardwired internet and power through MCC for any livestream, route every hung banner through the building's rigger, keep signage professional and freestanding, and get every crew member on the daily call list with a badge. Do those four things early and the cameras are the easy part.

If you want a crew that already plans this way, start with our Music City Center photographer page, which covers coverage options for conventions, conferences, galas and trade shows in the building.

What makes Music City Center different for media teams

The building is big, and its rules are written for big shows. The MCC fast facts list 2.1 million square feet in total, 1.2 million of it public space, a 353,143 square foot exhibit hall, two ballrooms, 60 meeting rooms and 145,000 square feet of pre-function space. One conference can run a keynote on Level 4, breakouts on Levels 1 and 2 and an expo on Level 3 at once, and your crew has to move between them with gear.

That is the first planning truth here: distance eats coverage. A photographer moving from a Level 1 breakout to a Level 4 award presentation needs the run of show, the routes and a buffer. Most coverage gaps at large venues are travel-time problems that nobody put on the schedule.

The second truth: power, internet and rigging come from the building, and those orders have deadlines. Nobody fixes a missing internet drop on show morning by plugging into a wall.

The exhibit hall

The hall can be divided into up to five sections: Hall A1 (27,500 square feet), A2 (50,900), B (87,000), C (87,000) and D (93,000), with room for 1,726 ten-by-ten booths across the full hall, per the MCC floor plans. The floor is scored in 10 by 10 foot sections, and the hall has 352 floor boxes on 30 foot centers carrying fiber and CAT6 data, plus more than 300 hang points rated to two tons each (Planning Guide).

For media, the floor box grid is where your hardwired internet and power come from, so put streaming and editing positions near one on the floor plan. And the ceiling is high enough that a ground-level camera makes a crowded expo look empty. Ask your Event Manager about a lift or a high position from the Show Manager Offices on Level 3M, which overlook the hall, for the money shot that sells next year's booths.

The Karl F. Dean Grand Ballroom

MCC describes the ballroom as designed to look like the inside of an acoustic guitar, and it photographs that way when the room is lit well. It has three main salons that divide into five breakout areas, a 78 foot wide stage with a 58 foot opening, and two green rooms (402 and 403) with restrooms and showers for speakers and talent (Planning Guide). The green rooms work well for a quick speaker portrait or interview.

In this room the camera plan is really a lighting plan. Stage light is set for the screens and the audience often sits in near dark, so ask the AV company for an audience wash during applause and awards.

Terraces and the green roof

The terraces are the only spaces where daylight is your key light. In mid-November, Nashville's sunset falls around 4:37 p.m. (timeanddate), so an evening reception on a terrace in late fall is a night shoot. Schedule outdoor group photos early in the event, and have a covered indoor alternative ready.

The four-acre green roof, planted with 14 types of sedum (MCC), is a strong visual for a sustainability message. Request access through your Event Manager.

Exclusive services and what they mean for your media vendor

The Planning Guide lists these exclusive services: electrical, compressed air, water and gas; EMTs during move-in, move-out and show hours; telecommunications, internet and cable or satellite connections; food and beverage; rigging; and move-in and move-out security.

Internet: order it hardwired for any livestream

MCC offers free facility-wide Wi-Fi, but its technology page says plainly that the free Wi-Fi is an unmanaged service, and that anyone who needs a higher level of connectivity should buy a wired service. A livestream is exactly that case. Order a dedicated hardwired line to your encoder position through MCC's technology team, test it on load-in day, and keep a bonded cellular unit as a backup. The building's infrastructure supports connection speeds in excess of 10 Gbps, so the capacity is there if you order it.

Power: plan drops for every station

Electrical service is exclusive too. The utilities page requires extension cords to be UL listed, labeled and appropriately sized. A headshot lounge with strobes, a livestream table, an editing station for same-day social posts and a charging point for camera batteries each need power, and each should be on the electrical order with a location on the floor plan.

Rigging: every hung banner goes through the building's rigger

Per the MCC rigging page, all rigging services are provided exclusively by Convention Production Rigging, Inc., including production rigging, exhibit rigging and graphic installations, inside and outside the building. The Planning Guide adds that any item attached to the building, including aisle signs, clings and floor decals, must go through MCC Rigging. If your sponsor wall, hanging banner or window cling is part of the media plan, the rigging order is part of the media plan.

Food and beverage

MCC has an exclusive caterer, and no outside food or beverage may be brought in apart from pre-approved samples (Planning Guide). For a long show day, budget crew meals through the building's catering or a meal allowance, so your photographer is at the door, not in a lunch line, when the governor walks in.

Audio-visual is not exclusive

MCC says audio-visual is not an exclusive service, though discounts are offered when the in-house provider is selected. LMG has been the preferred audio-visual partner since the facility opened in 2013, and rules for outside AV partners come from your Event Manager. In practice, your media team should get the AV company's contact early, ask for a clean audio feed from the board for every recorded session, and agree on camera risers and positions that do not block projection.

Signage, badges and the rules on the ground

Signage and decor rules

The Planning Guide's decorations and signage rules are strict, and they shape how you design print:

  • Signs must be professional quality. Handwritten signs are not permitted.
  • Banners and signs may not be attached, taped, nailed or otherwise fastened to any ceiling, window, equipment, painted surface or wall.
  • Do not drill, core or punch holes in the building.
  • Paper signs are limited to posterboard of eight square feet or less. Fabric signs are not size limited.
  • All decor and signs must sit at least six inches from MCC artwork.
  • Helium balloons and adhesive-backed decals (except nametags) are not allowed.

Easels and lecterns are provided complimentary, per the Planning Guide's list of services. So design directional and session signs for easels, build sponsor boards and step-and-repeats as freestanding pieces, and send anything that hangs to the rigger. A professionally designed and printed sign program costs less than reprinting on show morning because a sign got pulled. See our event graphics and printing page for sign programs built to these rules.

Drones are not allowed inside

The Planning Guide states that drones, hoverboards, skateboards, remote-controlled blimps and balloons are not allowed inside MCC. Aerial footage of the building or skyline is a separate, planned exterior shoot.

Badges, the call list and the contractor entrance

All individuals working at MCC must be on a daily call list and wear an approved identification badge, and a photo ID is required to get one. MCC participates in the ESCA Worker Identification and Security program (Planning Guide). All event personnel and service contractors must enter and leave through the entrance labeled "Contractor Entrance" on the south side of the building at 700 Korean Veterans Boulevard. Equipment cannot come through the main entrances without Event Manager approval.

Put every crew member on the call list for every day they work, with legal names that match their IDs. A missing name at the contractor entrance is the most avoidable delay in event media.

Loading docks, freight and parking

MCC has 32 loading docks, three drive-in ramps and three dock levelers, assigned through your Event Manager, and three freight elevators (two rated at 6,000 pounds and one at 12,000 pounds) scheduled by MCC. A printed backdrop, a headshot set or a livestream kit may need one.

The building's three-level covered garage at 701 Demonbreun Street has 1,800 spaces. Rates effective June 2, 2025 run $15 for up to five hours, $20 for five to nine hours, $25 for nine to twelve hours, $30 for twelve to eighteen hours and $45 overnight, with special event rates possible. There is no in and out on the same ticket, so tell your crew to park once and stay.

Planning timeline for Music City Center event media

The building's own client checklist sets the outer deadlines: certificate of insurance, floor plan and signage plan 30 days prior; all event requirements including room sets, security, rigging, AV and technology 14 days prior; and AV, technology and food and beverage quotes signed 10 to 14 days prior (Planning Guide). Your media plan has to be finished before those dates, not on them.

Planning for 2027 at Music City Center

The 2027 calendar is already crowded. Music City Center's public calendar lists dozens of 2027 conventions, trade shows and galas, including the Safari Club International Annual Convention (January 20 to 23), the Cattle Industry Convention and NCBA Trade Show (February 1 to 4), Dassault Systemes 3DEXPERIENCE World (February 14 to 17), ViVE (March 14 to 17), the Bitcoin Conference (July 15 to 17), Shoptalk Fall (October 5 to 7) and the Mortgage Bankers Association Annual Convention (October 17 to 20). We keep the full list, month by month, on our Music City Center events 2027 page.

If your event is on that calendar, book media the same season you sign the venue contract. The crews that know the building get held early for the big weeks, and every order that depends on them (power, hardwired internet, rigged banners, the call list) is easier to place when the media plan exists months before move-in. Exhibitors and sponsors heading to a 2027 show should book booth coverage as soon as their booth is confirmed.

Frequently asked questions

Are photographers already booking 2027 Music City Center events?

Yes. Planners book Music City Center years out, and media teams for the big 2027 weeks are being held now. If your 2027 dates are set, book photo, video and headshot coverage as soon as the venue contract is signed.

Can I bring my own photographer or videographer to Music City Center?

Yes. The building's exclusive services cover utilities, internet, rigging, food and beverage, EMTs and move-in and move-out security, not photography or video. Your photographer and videographer still follow the building's rules: they must be on the daily call list, wear an approved badge, use the contractor entrance at 700 Korean Veterans Boulevard and provide insurance paperwork when the show requires it.

Is audio-visual exclusive at Music City Center?

No. MCC states that audio-visual is not an exclusive service, though discounts apply when the in-house provider is used. LMG has been the preferred AV partner since 2013. Rules for outside AV companies come from your Event Manager.

Can you livestream from Music City Center on the free Wi-Fi?

You should not. MCC's own technology page calls the free Wi-Fi an unmanaged service and says users needing a higher level of connectivity should buy wired service. Order a hardwired internet line through MCC's exclusive technology team for any livestream or session upload, and test it on load-in day.

What are the signage rules at Music City Center?

Signs must be professional quality, and handwritten signs are not allowed. Nothing may be taped, nailed or otherwise fastened to walls, windows, ceilings or painted surfaces, and anything attached to the building goes through the exclusive rigging provider. Paper signs are capped at eight square feet, and easels and lecterns are provided complimentary.

Can you fly a drone inside Music City Center?

No. The Planning Guide says drones are not allowed inside MCC, along with hoverboards, skateboards, remote-controlled blimps and balloons. Aerial footage of the building or skyline has to be a separate, properly planned exterior shoot.

Where do vendors enter Music City Center?

All event personnel and service contractors enter and leave through the Contractor Entrance on the south side of the building at 700 Korean Veterans Boulevard. The main public address is 201 Rep. John Lewis Way South. Equipment cannot come through the main entrances without Event Manager approval.

What conventions are happening in Nashville at Music City Center?

The MCC calendar lists the National League of Cities City Summit (November 18 to 21, 2026), HYROX Nashville (December 9 to 13, 2026), the Safari Club International convention (January 20 to 23, 2027), ViVE (March 14 to 17, 2027) and The Bitcoin Conference (July 15 to 17, 2027), among many others. Check the calendar for current dates and expected attendance.

How far in advance should I book event media for a Music City Center show?

Book eight to twelve weeks out for a multi-day conference. MCC wants the insurance certificate and signage plan 30 days out and internet, power, rigging and AV requirements 14 days out, so settle your media plan before then.
