How Much Does a Videographer Cost Per Hour and Per Day?

2026 market rates for event, corporate and brand videographers, what the day rate actually buys, and how to compare quotes fairly.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

A professional videographer in the U.S. typically charges $60 to $100 an hour, or $600 to $1,200 for a full shoot day, at the mid-tier level, and $150 to $400 or more an hour ($2,000 to $3,500+ a day) for experienced specialists, according to Vidico's 2026 rate guide. Thumbtack puts the national average cost of hiring a videographer at $570 to $1,251 (updated October 2026), a figure that mostly reflects shorter local bookings. A crew of three to five people runs $2,500 to $8,000 a day, before editing. These are 2026 market ranges, not Leo Operations rates.

Videographer cost per hour

Experience is the biggest factor in hourly rates. A videographer who has filmed many conferences knows where to stand when the keynote speaker walks off stage, how to keep audio clean in a loud room, and how to shoot so the edit comes together quickly. Part of the rate pays for that judgment.

A few things to know about hourly quotes:

  • Many videographers set a minimum booking, often two to four hours, because travel, setup and breakdown take about the same time for a one-hour job as for a three-hour one.
  • Editing is often billed separately. Vidico lists a dedicated editor at $60 to $150 an hour, and Thumbtack shows a Bay Area example billing $95 an hour on site and $75 an hour for post-production.
  • Overtime is often billed at a higher rate than the base. Ask for the overtime rate in writing before the event.

How much does a videographer charge per day?

For a working professional, a full day of filming usually lands between $600 and $1,200 for one shooter with their own camera package, and $2,000 to $3,500 or more for a senior specialist, per Vidico's 2026 figures. A "day" in production is commonly 8 to 10 hours on site.

A half day is not half price. Vidico notes a half day usually costs 60% to 75% of the full day rate, because the shooter cannot sell the other half of that day to anyone else. On a $1,000 day rate, plan for $600 to $750 for a half day.

A day rate usually covers filming, not a finished video. Much of the cost of a polished piece is in editing, color, sound and graphics, which we break down in our video production cost guide.

Event videographer cost

Event video has one big difference from other shoots: nothing repeats. The CEO's announcement, the award moment and the surprise guest each happen once, so the crew has to be in position ahead of time.

Thumbtack's October 2026 data puts the national average cost of a videographer at $570 to $1,251, with a low end of $322 and a high end of $2,177. One provider quoted on that page lists basic personal event packages at $350 to $1,000 and nonprofit and corporate videos at $250 to $2,000. Numbers in this range generally fit smaller, shorter bookings, such as a birthday, a recital or a two-hour reception.

Corporate and institutional events usually cost more because they need more people and equipment. A conference, gala or awards night often needs:

  • Two or more cameras so the stage and the audience reaction are both covered.
  • A direct audio feed from the venue's sound board, plus wireless microphones as backup.
  • A shooter who can also capture interviews and b-roll between sessions.
  • A same-week highlight film for social, plus full-length session recordings.

Production companies usually price that work as a project. Levitate Media's 2026 guide lists corporate event video at $4,500 to $10,000 for a basic package, $10,000 to $15,000 for standard and $15,000 to $25,000 or more for premium multi-day coverage.

Corporate and brand videographer rates

Corporate shoots (interviews, testimonials, recruiting, product and brand films) are usually priced per project rather than per hour, because the work starts well before the shoot and continues well after it.

Vidico's 2026 data puts corporate and explainer videos at $4,500 to $20,000 per project and brand films or commercials at $15,000 to $50,000 or more. Per finished minute, testimonial and interview videos run $1,000 to $4,000 and corporate or training videos $1,000 to $10,000.

A corporate video costs more than the shooter's day rate because the shooter is one line in the budget. Scripting, interview prep, location scouting, lighting, a dedicated audio operator, editing, color grading, motion graphics, music licensing and revision rounds all add up. If a quote shows only a day rate, ask who is doing everything else.

For recruiting and employer brand work, our recruitment video guide covers what those projects need. For customer stories, see the testimonial video guide.

Why the hourly rate is not the whole picture

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics reports a median wage of $36.10 an hour for film and video editors and camera operators (May 2025), a figure that mostly reflects salaried employees. A freelancer's hourly rate is higher because it also has to cover:

  • Unbilled hours: emails, planning calls, travel, setup, file backup and delivery.
  • Equipment: cinema cameras, lenses, lights, wireless audio, stabilizers and backup bodies, all of which wear out and need replacing.
  • Insurance: general liability and equipment coverage, which many venues require.
  • Software and storage: editing suites, music licenses, and redundant drives for every project.
  • Editing time: on many projects, editing takes longer than filming.

When a quote is far below the market range, ask what it leaves out: backup equipment, a second camera, audio, or editing time. For a live event, gaps like these are hard to fix afterward, because nothing can be reshot.

Wedding videographer cost

Weddings are usually priced as packages built around a highlight film rather than by the hour. The Knot's 2025 Real Weddings Study puts the national average at about $2,300, and Zola's guide (updated March 2026) lists an average of $3,993. We break down those numbers, deliverables and add-ons in our wedding videographer cost guide.

How to compare videographer quotes

  1. Write down what you need delivered before you ask for a price: a 60-second social recap, a 3-minute highlight, full speeches, raw footage, or all of them.
  2. Ask every videographer for the same scope so the quotes are comparable.
  3. Watch two or three complete pieces from events like yours, with headphones on. Weak audio is one of the easiest problems to spot.
  4. Ask how many cameras and operators are included and who edits the footage.
  5. Get the turnaround time in writing for each deliverable.
  6. Ask about usage rights. For business work you want clear permission to use the video on your website, social channels, ads and presentations.
  7. Confirm insurance and whether the venue needs a certificate naming them.
  8. Ask how footage is backed up on site and how long archives are kept after delivery.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a videographer charge per day?

A mid-tier professional videographer typically charges $600 to $1,200 for a full shoot day in 2026, and senior specialists charge $2,000 to $3,500 or more, according to Vidico. A day usually means 8 to 10 hours on site. Editing is often priced separately, and a crew of three to five people runs $2,500 to $8,000 a day.

What is the average videographer cost per hour?

Vidico's 2026 figures put mid-tier professionals at $60 to $100 an hour, specialists at $150 to $400 or more, and entry-level shooters at $25 to $50. Many videographers also set a two to four hour minimum.

How much does an event videographer cost?

Thumbtack's October 2026 data shows a national average of $570 to $1,251, which mostly reflects short local bookings. Corporate events with multiple cameras, professional audio and a finished highlight film are priced as projects, commonly $4,500 to $10,000 for a basic package per Levitate Media's 2026 guide, with multi-day conferences running higher.

Is a half-day videographer rate cheaper?

Yes, but not by half. Vidico reports that half days usually cost 60% to 75% of the full day rate, since the shooter often cannot book another client that day. If you need more than four hours, compare the half-day and full-day quotes side by side.

Does the videographer's rate include editing?

Sometimes. Many event packages include a set edit, such as a highlight film, while hourly and day rates often cover filming only. Ask for editing to be listed as its own line with a clear deliverable and number of revision rounds.

What does a videographer do?

A videographer plans, films and often edits video of events, interviews and stories. On a professional job that includes scouting the location, setting up lights and microphones, capturing the moments and b-roll, managing files and delivering edited films. On larger productions those tasks are split among a director, camera operators, an audio tech and an editor.

Should I hire a freelancer or a production company?

A freelancer is a good fit for a single-camera shoot with a simple edit. Once you need multiple cameras, a dedicated audio operator, scripting or several deliverables on a deadline, a production company that manages the whole crew is usually easier to work with. You get one point of contact and a team used to working together.

How far in advance should I book a videographer?

For business events, book once the date and venue are confirmed. Conference and gala seasons are busy, and booking early leaves time for a walkthrough, an audio plan and a shot list.
