How to make a recruitment video that gets the right people to apply

Length by platform, the formats that work (employer brand, job-specific, day in the life, employee testimonials), real public examples, and where to post so candidates actually see it.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 12 min read

A recruitment video should run 60 to 120 seconds for a careers page or LinkedIn post, 15 to 30 seconds for paid social and vertical feeds, and 2 to 5 minutes only when it is a deeper employer brand film that a serious candidate chooses to watch. Indeed's own recruiting guidance points to two to five minutes for a full company video, while its paid Employer Branding Ads cap video at one minute. The videos that get people to apply show real employees doing the real job, say plainly what the work pays off in (growth, schedule, benefits, purpose), and end with one clear next step.

The rest of this guide covers which type of recruitment video to make first, a dozen ideas you can shoot, real public examples, how to plan a shoot, and where to post the finished cuts.

Why recruitment video moves applicants

Candidates research employers the way customers research products. LinkedIn's analysis of its own hiring data found that companies with a strong talent brand spend 43% less per hire and see a 31% higher InMail acceptance rate than companies without one. That finding is from 2015, but the logic has only gotten stronger as more of the job search has moved to phones and feeds.

Video is the fastest way to build that brand, because it answers the question a job post cannot: what is it actually like to work here. Wyzowl's 2026 State of Video Marketing survey found 85% of people say a video has convinced them to buy a product or service. A job is a bigger decision than most purchases. People want to see the building, hear the supervisor, and picture themselves on the team before they spend an hour on an application.

A good recruitment video also screens people out. When a warehouse role shows the 5 a.m. start and the pace of the floor, applicants already know what they are signing up for.

How long should a recruitment video be?

Length depends on where the video lives and what job it has to do. A single long film rarely works everywhere, so plan a main cut and several shorter versions from the same shoot.

VersionLengthWhere it runsJob it does
Employer brand film2 to 5 minutesCareers page, YouTube, info sessionsTells the full story for serious candidates
Main recruitment cut60 to 120 secondsLinkedIn, careers page, email to candidatesExplains the role, team and why to apply
Job-specific video45 to 90 secondsIndividual job posts, ATS listingShows one role, one manager, one workplace
Social cutdown15 to 30 secondsInstagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, paid adsStops the scroll and drives a click
Testimonial clip20 to 45 secondsSocial posts, recruiter outreachOne employee, one honest reason they stay

Platform limits are generous, so length is a choice, not a constraint. LinkedIn allows up to 15 minutes when uploading from desktop for member posts and up to 10 minutes for Page and Career Page videos. YouTube counts square or vertical videos up to three minutes as Shorts, and Instagram raised Reels to three minutes in January 2025. Just because you can post three minutes in a feed does not mean you should. Hook the viewer in the first two seconds or they are gone.

30 second recruitment videos

The 30 second version is the one most organizations underinvest in, and it is often the one that gets the most views. A strong 30 second recruitment video has a single idea: one employee, one moment of the job, one reason to apply. Open on action, not a logo. Put the role and location on screen early, add captions (most feed video plays muted), and end on a short URL or "Apply at" card. Shoot it vertical, or frame the main film so a 9:16 crop still works.

Employer brand video vs. job-specific video

These are two different tools, and most organizations need both.

An employer brand video sells the organization as a place to build a career. It covers mission, culture, growth paths, benefits and the people. It stays useful for two or three years, so it deserves the most production investment: multiple locations, good lighting and sound, drone or motion shots where they help, and a polished edit with licensed music.

A job-specific video sells one role. The hiring manager explains what the person will do in their first 90 days, a current team member shows the workspace, and the video states the schedule and the must-haves. These are faster to shoot and should be refreshed whenever the role changes. For high-volume roles (nurses, drivers, technicians, teachers, call center staff) a job-specific video can do more for applications than the brand film, because it answers the practical questions candidates actually have.

A smart shoot plan captures both in the same production days. Film the brand interviews and B-roll, then spend an hour per priority role on job-specific footage.

Recruitment video ideas that work

Here are formats we see work across industries. Pick two or three that fit your roles rather than trying all of them at once.

  1. Day in the life. Follow one employee from arrival to end of shift. Keep it honest: the meetings, the hard part, the part they love. This is the format candidates trust most because it looks unscripted.
  2. Employee testimonials. Three to five employees answer the same questions on camera. Why they joined, what surprised them, why they stay. Cut the best answers together.
  3. Meet the manager. The person the new hire will report to explains how they run the team and what success looks like. Candidates choose managers as much as companies.
  4. Career path stories. An employee who started in an entry role and moved up explains how. This is powerful for front-line and hourly hiring.
  5. Facility or site tour. Walk the space with a team member narrating. Especially useful for hospitals, plants, campuses and anywhere the physical environment matters.
  6. "What we wish we knew" video. Recent hires share what they wish they had known before day one. Builds trust and filters for fit.
  7. Benefits explained by people who use them. A parent talks about the schedule flexibility, a nurse about tuition support. Far more persuasive than a bullet list.
  8. Training program film. Show the onboarding or apprenticeship from the first day to graduation.
  9. Community and mission piece. Show the impact of the work on the people you serve.
  10. Leadership welcome. A short, sincere message from the CEO, executive director or principal. Keep it under a minute.
  11. Interview process walkthrough. A recruiter explains each step and how long it takes. Reduces drop-off from nervous candidates.
  12. Event recap. A hiring event, job fair or open house cut into a fast 30 to 60 second piece that invites people to the next one.

The thread through all of these is real people. Stock footage and actors read as fake to job seekers in about two seconds.

Recruitment video examples worth studying

The best recruitment videos share a pattern: real staff, a clear audience and a specific emotional promise. Two large public campaigns show the range.

NHS England, "We are the NHS." Launched in 2018 for the health service's 70th anniversary, the campaign used TV and online films featuring real NHS staff to recruit nurses. NHS England reported in February 2019 that 30,650 people applied for nursing degrees that year, up 4.3% on 2018, reversing a decline, with applications from men also rising. The lesson: put the people who do the job at the center, and speak directly to the groups you are short on.

The British Army, "Your Army Needs You" (2019). Created by the agency Karmarama, the campaign ran six posters and three TV adverts that took labels aimed at young people (gamers, "snowflakes," "selfie addicts") and reframed them as strengths. The Army later reported applications at a five-year high in early 2019. It also drew criticism, and the number of people actually joining did not rise as fast as applications. The lesson: a bold creative idea gets attention, and the videos and process after the click have to carry candidates all the way to day one.

How to plan and shoot a recruitment video

Start with the hiring problem

Write down which roles you are struggling to fill, who the ideal candidate is, and what usually makes good candidates say no. That last answer is your script. If people turn down the job because of the commute, show the shuttle or the parking. If they worry about being the only one, show the team.

Cast employees, not volunteers who love the camera

Ask managers to nominate people who are good at the job and respected by peers. Aim for a cast that reflects the range of people you want to hire. Prepare them with the questions in advance, not a script. Memorized lines sound memorized.

Interview questions that produce usable answers

  • What were you doing before you came here, and why did you make the move?
  • Walk me through a normal day.
  • What is the hardest part of the job, and how does the team handle it?
  • What surprised you after you started?
  • Tell me about someone here who helped you grow.
  • What would you say to a friend thinking about applying?

Ask for answers in full sentences that include the question, so each clip stands on its own.

Production standards that matter

Sound is the first thing that makes a recruitment video look amateur. Use lavalier microphones on every speaker and shoot interviews in a quiet room. Light faces properly. Capture plenty of B-roll of people working, because B-roll is what lets you cut a 90 second film down to 30 seconds without losing the story. Get a signed release from everyone who appears on camera (our testimonial video guide covers release forms in detail). Use licensed music only, a point Indeed's guidance also stresses.

This is where professional production earns its cost. A crew that knows how to make nervous employees comfortable, light a warehouse, and record clean audio on a loud floor will give you footage that holds up for years and across a dozen cutdowns.

Where to post a recruitment video

A recruitment video only works if candidates see it. Plan distribution before you shoot.

  • Careers page. Put the main cut at the top of the page, above the job list. Add job-specific videos to each role's page.
  • LinkedIn. Post native video (uploaded directly, not a YouTube link) on your Company Page and Career Page, and ask featured employees to share it.
  • Job boards. Indeed lets employers add video to Company Pages and, through a sales rep, to Employer Branding Ads with a one-minute limit.
  • Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and YouTube Shorts. Vertical 15 to 30 second cuts with captions. These reach younger and hourly candidates who never open LinkedIn.
  • Recruiter outreach. Recruiters can link a short video in emails and texts to candidates. It warms up a cold message.
  • Hiring events and info sessions. Play the brand film on screens at job fairs, campus visits and open houses.
  • Paid social. Boost your best-performing cut to a targeted audience by location and interest.

Track views, click-throughs to the application, and application completion. Then re-cut the version that works into new variations.

Recruitment videos for students and schools

Recruitment video also means enrollment video: schools, colleges, universities, training programs and apprenticeships recruiting students. The same principles apply, with a few differences.

Students and parents are both your audience. Students want to see other students, campus life and what they will be doing day to day. Parents want to see safety, outcomes and the teachers or instructors. A strong school recruitment film gives each audience at least one moment that speaks directly to them.

Let students tell the story. Current students and recent graduates are more persuasive than any administrator. For career and technical programs, show the path from training to a real job with real equipment. For charter and private schools, show classrooms in action and families at pickup, not empty hallways.

Timing matters. Enrollment and application seasons are fixed, so plan the shoot months before your open house and application deadlines, while classes are in session and campus looks alive.

For college athletic and organization recruiting (including the sorority and club recruitment videos many students search for), the same rules hold: real members, real moments, and a clear invitation to the next event.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a recruitment video be?

For a careers page or LinkedIn, aim for 60 to 120 seconds. For paid social and vertical feeds, 15 to 30 seconds. A full employer brand film can run 2 to 5 minutes, which matches Indeed's guidance for company videos. Shoot once and cut several versions.

What are good recruitment video ideas?

Day-in-the-life videos, employee testimonials, meet-the-manager clips, career path stories and facility tours are the most reliable. Benefits explained by employees who use them and a walkthrough of the interview process also perform well. Every idea works better with real employees than with actors.

What makes the best recruitment videos?

The best recruitment videos feature real employees, speak to a specific audience, show the actual work, and end with a clear call to apply. Clean audio, good lighting and captions matter, because poor production makes an employer look careless to the very people it wants to impress.

Are 30 second recruitment videos effective?

Yes, for social feeds and paid ads they are often the most-watched version. Keep them to one idea, open with action, put the role and location on screen, add captions and end with where to apply. Use them to drive people to the longer video and the job post.

What is the difference between an employer brand video and a recruitment video?

An employer brand video sells the organization as a place to work and stays useful for years. A job-specific recruitment video sells one role, with the hiring manager and team, and should be updated as the role changes. Most organizations need one brand film and several job-specific videos.

Where should I post my recruitment video?

Start with your careers page and LinkedIn Company and Career Pages, then post vertical cutdowns to Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and YouTube Shorts. Add video to job board company pages, use it in recruiter emails, and play it at hiring events.

How do you make a recruitment video for students?

Put current students and recent graduates on camera, show real classrooms and campus life, and give parents the information they care about. Shoot while school is in session and finish the edit before your open house and application season.

Do employees need to sign a release to appear in a recruitment video?

Yes. Get a signed video release from every employee, student or customer who appears on camera, and from a parent or guardian for minors. State laws on using a person's likeness in advertising vary, and New York, for example, requires written consent.
