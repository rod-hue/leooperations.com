Ballot initiative signature requirements and deadlines, state by state

How many valid signatures each initiative state demands, where they have to come from, when they are due, and what changes now that the 2026 votes are in.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated November 2026 · 11 min read

Each state sets its own signature number for a ballot initiative, almost always as a percentage of a recent vote. For the 2026 ballot, California required 546,651 valid signatures for a statute and 874,641 for a constitutional amendment, Florida required 880,062 for an amendment, and Colorado required 124,238, according to Ballotpedia's state chart. Montana required 30,121 for a statute (Montana Free Press). Only 26 states plus Washington, D.C. have a statewide citizen initiative or veto referendum process at all. Most of the requirements reset after a governor's race, so many of these numbers will change for 2028 once the November 3, 2026 results are certified.

The raw total is only the first hurdle. Distribution rules, collection windows and signature verification can sink a drive that clears the headline number.

How many signatures are needed for a ballot initiative?

Every initiative state ties its requirement to a base number. The most common base is the total vote for governor in the last election. Others use the vote for secretary of state, the presidential vote, the number of registered voters, or the state's population.

  • Votes for governor: Arizona, Arkansas, California, Maine, Massachusetts, Michigan, Missouri and Montana all use it. California, for example, requires 8 percent of the governor vote for an amendment and 5 percent for a statute (California Secretary of State).
  • Votes for secretary of state: Colorado requires 5 percent of the votes cast for secretary of state in the preceding general election (Ballotpedia).
  • Presidential vote: Florida requires 8 percent of the votes cast in the last presidential election (Ballotpedia).
  • Registered voters: Idaho uses 6 percent of registered voters as of the last general election (Ballotpedia News). Nebraska counts registered voters at the filing deadline, so petitioners cannot know their exact target until they turn in signatures (Ballotpedia).

Amendments need more signatures than statutes in nearly every state that offers both (in Michigan, 446,198 versus 356,958). Decide early which kind you are running, because that choice sets the size of the whole field program.

Read the Nevada row twice. Indirect statutes there are filed 15 days after a general election and go to the next legislature. They reach the ballot two years later. A 2028 Nevada statute is due to the counties November 18, 2026 (Nevada Secretary of State).

Collection windows

The deadline is half the story. The other half is how long a signature stays good.

In Michigan, all signatures must be gathered within a 180-day window (Bridge Michigan).

A short window like Michigan's changes the staffing math. You cannot spread 450,000 signatures over two years with volunteers. You need a paid canvass at full speed for six months.

Signature distribution requirements: where the signatures have to come from

Distribution rules stop a campaign from qualifying on one metro area alone. Rural counties and districts are slow and expensive to work, and that is where drives get into trouble.

StateDistribution requirement
AlaskaA minimum share of the district vote in at least 30 of the 40 House districts (Alaska Lieutenant Governor)
ArkansasHalf the required percentage in each of at least 50 counties (Arkansas handbook)
Idaho6% of registered voters in 18 of 35 legislative districts (Ballotpedia News)
MassachusettsNo more than 25% of certified signatures from any one county (Ballotpedia)
MissouriThe percentage must be met in 6 of the 8 congressional districts (Missouri Secretary of State)
MontanaAmendments: 10% of qualified electors in 40 of 100 legislative districts; statutes: 5% in 34 House districts (Ballotpedia, Montana Free Press)
Nebraska5% of registered voters in 38 counties (Lancaster County Election Commission)

Arkansas raised its bar from 15 counties to 50. A judge ruled the 50-county law unconstitutional and the state appealed to the Arkansas Supreme Court, so 2026 campaigns kept collecting in 50 counties as a precaution (University of Arkansas Cooperative Extension, Arkansas Ballot Issues Newsletter). A drive that hits its statewide total with thin numbers in a dozen counties fails. Build county or district targets on day one and track them weekly, separately from the statewide count.

How many signatures are needed for a ballot initiative in California?

For 2026, California required 874,641 valid signatures for a constitutional amendment and 546,651 for a statute or referendum. Those equal 8 and 5 percent of the 2022 governor vote (California Secretary of State). The secretary of state must certify a measure at least 131 days before the election, which was June 25, 2026.

Counties verify by random sample first. Below 95 percent of the requirement, the measure fails. Above 110 percent, it qualifies. In between, every signature gets checked, so serious campaigns file early and well past the minimum.

The 2028 figures will be based on the November 3, 2026 governor vote, so lock your target when the state publishes it.

Why you need a signature cushion

Officials strike signatures when the signer is not registered or is registered in the wrong county. They also strike duplicate signatures and sheets where the circulator made an error. Look at what 2026 drives turned in:

Campaign (2026)Valid signatures requiredSignatures submitted
Missouri, Respect Missouri Voters amendment177,713 (per the campaign's lawsuit)More than 367,000 (Missouri Independent)
Idaho, two statutory initiatives70,725 eachMore than 100,000 each (Ballotpedia News)

The Missouri case carries a second lesson. Secretary of State Denny Hoskins rejected that petition on single-subject grounds, not for lack of signatures. The campaign sued. The Missouri Supreme Court ruled unanimously that he lacked the authority to reject it, and voters approved it as Amendment 6 on November 3, 2026 (ABC17, KY3). Get legal review of the text before you print.

A good planning rule: aim for at least 30 percent above the requirement, and more where distribution rules are tight. Sample your own validity rate every week so you know where you stand long before filing day.

What changes for 2028

Three things will move the numbers and rules before the 2028 deadlines.

  1. New base numbers. Every state tied to the 2026 governor vote will recalculate, including Arizona, Arkansas, California, Maine, Massachusetts and Michigan. Colorado's number resets on the 2026 secretary of state vote. Florida's 880,062 is based on the 2024 presidential vote and holds through 2028. Montana and Washington elected governors in 2024, so their governor-based figures should hold until those offices are on the ballot again. Nevada petitions filed in 2026 already use the 2024 vote: 148,788 signatures (Nevada Secretary of State).
  2. New process laws and court fights. The Department of State announced that no citizen initiative made Florida's 2026 ballot (Florida Politics). State records showed the marijuana amendment about 100,000 signatures short after the deadline (CBS News Miami). The appeal over Arkansas's 50-county rule could change the map there.
  3. Amendments voters just decided. On August 4, 2026, Missouri voters rejected Amendment 4 by 80 to 20 percent. It would have required initiated amendments to win a majority in every congressional district (Missouri Independent). On November 3, they approved Amendment 6, which requires an 80 percent vote in both chambers before lawmakers change a voter-approved statute (KY3). North Dakota voters rejected Constitutional Measure 1, which would have required a 60 percent vote for constitutional amendments (Ballotpedia, MultiState). Check the certified results before you write a 2028 plan in those states.

Frequently asked questions

How many signatures are needed for a ballot initiative?

It depends on the state and the type of measure. For the 2026 ballot, a Montana statute needed 30,121 and a Florida amendment needed 880,062, per Ballotpedia. Most states set the number as a percentage of the last governor vote, and amendments need more than statutes.

How many signatures are needed for a ballot initiative in California?

For 2026, 874,641 valid signatures for a constitutional amendment and 546,651 for a statute (California Secretary of State). Those equal 8 and 5 percent of the 2022 governor vote. The 2028 numbers will be based on the 2026 governor vote once it is certified.

How many signatures are needed to get an initiative on a ballot in Texas?

Texas has no statewide citizen initiative, so there is no statewide number. It is not among the 26 states on Ballotpedia's list. Local petitions, such as city charter amendments, follow city charters and Texas law. Confirm the local rule with your city secretary before collecting.

Which states allow ballot initiatives?

According to Ballotpedia, 26 states and Washington, D.C. have a process for citizen initiatives, veto referendums or both. Twenty-four states allow citizen initiatives (Bridge Michigan). Some, like Florida and Illinois, allow only constitutional amendments, while Alaska, Idaho, Maine and others allow only statutes.

Do people who sign a petition have to be registered voters?

In California, yes (California Secretary of State), and the same rule applies in the large majority of initiative states. Signatures from unregistered people or voters registered at an old address are among the most common reasons signatures get thrown out.

How are initiative signatures verified?

Many states sample. Colorado certifies a measure if the sample projects more than 110 percent of the requirement and rejects it below 90 percent. In between, it does a full count (Ballotpedia). California's floor is 95 percent.

How many extra signatures should you collect?

Plan for at least 30 percent above the requirement, more where distribution rules are strict. 2026 drives in Missouri and Idaho submitted roughly 41 to 107 percent more than the minimum.

Can you sign a ballot petition online?

Not in most states. Initiatives to allow electronic signatures were filed in Florida and Missouri for 2026. That tells you the default is still a handwritten signature on an approved form.
