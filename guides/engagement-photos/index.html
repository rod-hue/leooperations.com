Engagement Photos: What They Cost, What to Wear and How Not to Look Awkward

A practical guide to engagement sessions for couples (and the parents who keep asking when they will see them), from budget and timing to outfits, posing and save-the-dates.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

Engagement photos typically cost $300 to $1,500 for a one to two hour session as a market range, and many wedding photographers include a session free or discounted when you book them for the wedding. Schedule the shoot 6 to 9 months before the wedding, early enough to use the photos on save-the-dates, which The Knot says should go out no later than six months before the wedding. Wear solid, mid-tone colors that coordinate without matching, and pick a place that means something to you.

The engagement session has a second job most couples do not realize until afterward. It is a rehearsal. You learn how your photographer works, they learn which side of your face you prefer and how your partner laughs, and you both stop being stiff in front of a lens. On the wedding day, that is worth a lot.

How much should I expect to spend on engagement photos?

Pricing depends on your market, the photographer's experience and session length. Sample listings on Zola's vendor marketplace run from about $100 for a short off-peak session with a newer photographer to $1,100 for a two hour "adventure" session with an established studio in a big city. A broader cost guide from Fash puts typical sessions at $300 to $1,500.

Is $500 a lot for engagement photos?

Not for a standalone session with an experienced professional in most US cities. It sits in the middle of the market range. If $500 is an add-on to a wedding package that already costs several thousand dollars, ask whether they will include it, discount it, or credit it toward an album. Many will.

Whatever a photographer says about an included or discounted session, confirm it is written into your contract or package terms before you count on it. Hidden costs to check: location permits (some parks and botanical gardens charge), travel fees beyond a set radius, extra outfit changes, rush editing, and prints.

When should you take engagement photos?

Six to nine months before the wedding is the sweet spot for most couples. Set the exact date by working backward from your own calendar: when your save-the-dates need to go to the printer (earlier for destination weddings, where guests need more notice to book travel), when your photographer is booked, and when your chosen location looks its best.

  • Too early (right after the proposal) and you may not have booked your wedding photographer yet, which wastes the rehearsal value.
  • Too late (within 3 months) and you miss save-the-date deadlines and add stress during the busiest planning stretch.
  • Season matters. Fall color and spring blooms are the most requested, and those weekends fill quickly. Golden hour (the hour before sunset) gives the softest light in almost every location.
  • Hair and makeup trial: many brides schedule their wedding hair and makeup trial the same day. You get two things done and see how the look photographs.

Engagement photo locations that actually work

The best location is one that tells your story and has enough variety for 60 to 90 minutes of shooting. Some reliable choices:

  • Where you met, got engaged or had your first date. A coffee shop, a campus, a neighborhood.
  • A city setting. Murals, bridges, rooftops, old brick. Downtown Nashville, for example, gives you neon, river views and quiet side streets within a few blocks.
  • Nature. Parks, lakes, fields, beaches. Check whether the park requires a commercial photo permit.
  • Your home. Coffee in the kitchen, the dog on the couch. Lifestyle sessions at home often produce the most natural photos of the year.
  • Doing something you love. Cooking, a record store, a basketball court, a farmers market, your church steps.
  • Cultural or family places. A grandparent's home, a family restaurant, a neighborhood with meaning to your heritage.

Two locations is usually the right number. More than that and you spend the session driving.

What to wear for engagement photos

What is the best color to wear for engagement photos?

Solid, medium-tone colors photograph best: dusty blue, sage, rust, burgundy, cream, camel, navy, olive and soft earth tones. They flatter most skin tones and do not distract from faces. Coordinate rather than match. If one person wears navy, the other can wear cream or rust. Pick a palette of two or three colors and stay inside it.

Think about the background. Bright jewel tones pop against a neutral city wall. Earth tones blend into a fall forest for a soft, cohesive look, so add one deeper color (burgundy, navy) if you want to stand out from the leaves.

What to avoid for engagement photos

  • Small, tight patterns (pinstripes, tiny checks, houndstooth) that shimmer on camera
  • Big logos and graphic text
  • Neon colors that cast color onto skin
  • Head-to-toe matching outfits
  • Anything brand new that you have not worn long enough to know it fits well sitting down
  • Shoes you cannot walk in for 20 minutes on grass or gravel

A subtle pattern on one person, like a floral dress or a textured knit, works fine if the other person wears a solid.

Women's outfits and the long vs short dress question

Should you wear a long or short dress for engagement photos? Both work. A long, flowing dress reads as romantic and moves beautifully in walking shots and wind, which suits fields, beaches and golden hour. A short or midi dress looks more modern and suits city settings. Many women bring one of each. Whatever you choose, make sure you can sit, kneel and walk in it comfortably. Also bring a layer for cool evenings and a pair of flat shoes for walking between spots.

Men's outfits

Men get the least advice and often look the most uncomfortable. Easy formula: well-fitted chinos or dark jeans, a solid button-down or fine knit sweater, clean shoes or boots. For a dressier look, a suit with no tie or an unstructured blazer. Avoid shirts so loose they billow or so tight they pull at the buttons. Get a haircut about a week before, not the day before.

For couples incorporating cultural attire, an outfit change into traditional clothing (an agbada and gele, a guntiino, a lehenga, a kente piece) creates some of the most striking images of the session. Tell your photographer in advance so they plan light and location around it.

How to not look awkward in engagement photos

Nobody is naturally comfortable being photographed for an hour. Good photographers fix this with prompts (small instructions that create real moments) instead of stiff poses.

Prompts that work:

  • "Walk toward me and whisper something that will make them laugh."
  • "Hold hands and walk away from me, slowly. Look at each other, not at me."
  • "Tell each other what you thought the first time you met."
  • "Forehead to forehead, eyes closed, breathe."
  • "Slow dance. No music needed."
  • "Partner A, try to make Partner B laugh without words."

What are the top 10 engagement photo poses?

These are the classics most photographers use as starting points:

  1. Walking hand in hand toward the camera
  2. Walking away, looking back
  3. Forehead touch
  4. Back hug (one partner wraps arms around the other from behind)
  5. Almost-kiss
  6. Seated together on steps or a bench, leaning in
  7. Piggyback ride
  8. Dip or twirl
  9. Ring close-up with hands layered
  10. Laughing together, looking at each other instead of the camera

Practical tips: keep your hands busy (hold each other, a coat, a coffee), keep a little bend in your arms and knees, leave space between your arm and your body, and look at your partner more than at the lens. The first 15 minutes always feel strange. By minute 20 most couples forget the camera.

Using engagement photos for save-the-dates and wedding websites

Plan the shoot with the uses in mind:

  • Save-the-dates: you need at least one horizontal and one vertical image with clean negative space for text. Tell the photographer.
  • Wedding website: a wide hero image plus 8 to 15 storytelling shots for an "Our Story" page.
  • Welcome sign and guest book: a large print or a small photo album guests sign.
  • Social announcements: a few vertical crops for Instagram and stories.
  • Gifts for parents: a framed print is an easy, meaningful thank-you.

Ask for a few sneak-peek images early if your save-the-date printer has a deadline.

Engagement video ideas

A short engagement film (60 to 180 seconds) is growing in popularity and plays well on wedding websites and at the reception.

  • Love story interview: sit together, answer a few questions on camera (how you met, the proposal), cut with B-roll of you walking around your city.
  • Proposal recreation: revisit the place and replay the moment.
  • Day-in-the-life: a morning routine, a favorite restaurant, an evening walk.
  • Letters: each partner writes a short letter, reads it aloud privately, and the edit uses the audio as voiceover.
  • Family message: parents or grandparents say a few words, a lovely piece to play at the rehearsal dinner.

If you are hiring a videographer for the wedding anyway, an engagement film also gives you a preview of their editing style. For more on wedding film styles, see our guide to wedding videography styles.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I expect to spend on engagement photos?

Most couples spend $300 to $1,500 for a standalone session, depending on location and photographer experience. Many wedding photographers include an engagement session free or at a discount when you book the wedding. Ask before paying separately, and make sure the inclusion is written into your contract.

What is the best color to wear for engagement photos?

Solid, medium-tone colors such as dusty blue, sage, rust, burgundy, navy, cream and earth tones work best for most skin tones. Coordinate with your partner in a two or three color palette rather than matching exactly. Avoid neon and tiny patterns.

How to not look awkward in engagement photos?

Choose a photographer who uses prompts instead of rigid poses, keep your hands engaged, and focus on your partner instead of the camera. Walking, whispering and laughing together reads more natural than standing still. Expect the first 15 minutes to feel stiff, then it eases.

What to avoid for engagement photos?

Avoid small busy patterns, logos, neon colors, fully matching outfits, and shoes you cannot walk in. Also avoid scheduling at midday in bright sun, which creates harsh shadows. Golden hour is far kinder.

Should I wear a long or short dress for engagement photos?

Either works. Long dresses look romantic and move well in outdoor shots, while short or midi dresses suit city settings. Many women bring one of each for an outfit change.

When should we take engagement photos?

Six to nine months before the wedding works for most couples. Work backward from your save-the-date print deadline (save-the-dates should go out at least six months ahead, and earlier for destination weddings) so the edited photos arrive in time, and you also get a rehearsal with your wedding photographer.

Is $500 a lot for engagement photos?

No, $500 is mid-range for a standalone professional session in most US markets. If it is an add-on to your wedding package, ask whether it can be included or discounted.
