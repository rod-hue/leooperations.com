The Best Christmas Photo Spots in Nashville (2026 Family Guide)

Verified 2026 dates for Nashville's holiday light shows, Santa photo sets and free outdoor backdrops, plus the photo rules most families never hear about until they get there.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated November 2026 · 11 min read

The best Christmas photo spots in Nashville for 2026 are Gaylord Opryland's A Country Christmas (November 6, 2026 to January 2, 2027), Cheekwood's Holiday LIGHTS (November 20 to January 3), Nashville Zoo's new Glow Wild (November 13 to January 3) and Franklin's Dickens of a Christmas on Main Street (December 12 and 13). For Santa, the malls run sets from early November to Christmas Eve, and Nashville Yards offers free Santa photos on select December weekends. For a planned family portrait with a hired photographer, the strongest plan pairs a daytime session at a permitted location with an evening visit to a light show for candids.

Every date below was checked against the venue or a named local source on November 17, 2026. Holiday schedules change, so confirm tickets before you load the car seats. If you want a professional to handle the location, the permit and the light, our Nashville Christmas photos page explains how we run holiday sessions.

Gaylord Opryland: the classic Nashville Christmas picture

If you ask a Nashville family what to do for Christmas, Opryland is usually the first answer, and for photos it earns that spot. The 43rd annual A Country Christmas runs November 6, 2026 through January 2, 2027, and this year's ICE! is themed to Home Alone, carved from more than two million pounds of ice. The same announcement lists a reimagined Cirque show, a new Secret Santa Scavenger Hunt, Nutmeg's Holiday Kitchen Character Breakfast, a Gingerbread Decorating Corner and the Delta Riverboat Christmas Cruise.

Opryland is the best answer to the question of indoor places to take pictures in Nashville in December. The glass atriums are climate controlled, so a toddler in a velvet dress does not have to fight 35 degree wind. Santa photos at Gaylord run November 6 to December 24, with packages from $45 and tickets required.

Photo tips for Opryland:

  • Go on a weekday afternoon. Weekend evenings in December are packed, and crowds fill the background of every wide shot.
  • ICE! is kept at 9 degrees Fahrenheit, with complimentary parkas for kids and adults, so plan your ICE! photos as quick candids in the parkas they hand out, and save the dressed-up family portrait for the atriums.
  • The atrium light is mixed (skylights, tungsten, colored LEDs). Turn off your flash and let the decorations light faces. Phones handle this well if you tap to expose on a face.

Cheekwood Holiday LIGHTS and the 3:30 PM rule

Cheekwood is the prettiest light show in town for adults and older kids. Holiday LIGHTS runs November 20, 2026 to January 3, 2027, 4:30 to 10 pm, with a member preview night on November 19. Gardens-only adult tickets are $26 on non-peak nights and $29 on peak nights (Fridays, Saturdays and the week before Christmas). Youth 3 to 17 run $20 to $23, and children 2 and under are free. Military Mondays (November 23 and 30, December 7 and 28) are half off.

Here is the rule that trips up families who plan to book a photographer there. Cheekwood's Photography and Videography Guidelines say that during November and December through the end of Holiday LIGHTS in January, all booked portrait session guests must be off the grounds by 3:30 PM, with no after-hours sessions. The same guidelines ban props on the grounds (balloons, candles, sparklers, confetti and similar), keep sessions out of the mansion and other buildings, and require every posed session to be booked through the Special Events Office.

Booked sessions cost $450 for members or $550 for non-members for two hours, include admission for up to six guests, and need three days' notice. So the Cheekwood plan is a daytime portrait session in the gardens, or an evening of LIGHTS with your own camera for candids. You cannot book a professional session under the lights.

Cheekwood also hosts daytime Visits with Santa from December 12 to 24 (Tuesday to Friday 10 am to 2 pm, weekends 10 am to 3 pm) and Dog Visits with Santa on December 13 and December 22, all with garden admission.

Nashville Zoo Glow Wild (the new show for 2026)

Zoolumination, the lantern festival many families remember, was canceled for 2026 because of visa issues affecting its event partner. In its place the zoo is running Glow Wild, nightly November 13, 2026 to January 3, 2027, 5 to 9 pm. Single-night tickets are $27 (members save $5), a Platinum Pass for unlimited visits is $39, and children under 2 are free. Advance tickets are required.

The photo anchors are the 85-foot synchronized Christmas tree and an outdoor ice rink ($12, or $10 for members). Santa is scheduled for select nights from November 27 to December 24. For little kids, Glow Wild is the strongest pick on this list: it closes at 9, which means a 5:15 arrival gets you full darkness and glowing backdrops before bedtime meltdowns.

Franklin's Dickens of a Christmas and other small-town spots

When someone asks for the most Christmassy town near Nashville, Franklin is the answer we give. The 41st annual Dickens of a Christmas takes over downtown Franklin on Saturday, December 12 and Sunday, December 13, 2026, starting at 10 am, with characters from A Christmas Carol and Oliver Twist and a Victorian Father and Mother Christmas. The storefronts, brick sidewalks and costumed carolers make a ready-made set, and the festival crowd adds energy to candids.

Arrive at opening. By early afternoon, Main Street is shoulder to shoulder and the clean storefront shot is gone. Any other weekday in December, Main Street is still decorated and nearly empty in the morning, which is when we would schedule a posed family session.

Two historic sites round out the list:

  • Andrew Jackson's Hermitage runs Christmas by Lamplight tours at 6 pm on Fridays and Saturdays from November 13 to December 19, plus December 26, and a Jacksonian Christmas Festival at Tulip Grove on December 5.
  • Belle Meade Historic Site offers holiday family tours from mid-November through December. Portrait sessions there need a Photography Pass reserved in advance, the grounds are open 9 am to 5 pm, and photography inside the mansion is strictly prohibited.

Free outdoor Christmas photo spots in Nashville

You do not need a ticket for every great holiday photo. These free backdrops work well in December:

  • Downtown and Broadway. The Nashvillage Holiday Market fills Walk of Fame Park on December 3 to 6 and 10 to 13. Shoot at blue hour, about 20 minutes after sunset, when the sky is deep blue and the neon reads warm.
  • The Gulch. The Gulch Holiday Market runs Sunday, December 13, 11 am to 3 pm at Canopy by Hilton. Shop windows and string lights along the side streets make easy backdrops on any December evening.
  • Nashville Yards. Free Santa photo weekends (listed above) make it an easy downtown stop with kids.
  • Centennial Park and the Parthenon. The Parthenon is the most recognizable building in town, and photography is welcome inside the museum except at one display case in the Centennial Gallery. That makes it another strong indoor option on a cold or rainy day.
  • Franklin Main Street. Decorated all season and free to walk.

Do you need a permit for Christmas photos in Nashville?

Families taking their own pictures do not need a permit. A professional you hire often does, and it is worth asking before you book.

Metro Parks covers Centennial Park, Shelby Bottoms, the Warner Parks and every other Metro park. Its film, video and photography permit page requires a permit for commercial photography, defined as recording images for a market audience with the intent of generating income. The fees:

Metro Parks photography permitDavidson CountyOut of county
Daily$125$150
Annual (12 months, all Metro parks)$250$300

Applications filed with less than 48 hours' notice may not be granted, a certificate of insurance is required, props and extensive setup on greenspace require a separate greenspace rental, and drones are not allowed outside designated flying areas. Cheekwood and Belle Meade run their own systems, covered above.

Ask any photographer you are considering whether they hold the right permit for the location. A professional will answer that question without hesitation and build the fee into the plan.

How to plan a Nashville Christmas photo day

  1. Pick one anchor. One light show or one Santa set per outing. Families who try Opryland and the zoo in one night end up with tired kids in both.
  2. Book the posed portrait in daylight. Professional sessions at places like Cheekwood have to wrap by 3:30 PM, and daylight gives the cleanest skin tones for cards.
  3. Do the lights at blue hour. Arrive just as the show opens so you get the glow without the full crowd.
  4. Reserve Santa slots now. Weekday morning slots go first.
  5. Dress for the cold, then peel layers. Keep coats on until the shutter is ready.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best Christmas photo spots in Nashville for families?

For 2026, the top family picks are Gaylord Opryland's A Country Christmas (November 6 to January 2), Nashville Zoo's Glow Wild (November 13 to January 3), Cheekwood Holiday LIGHTS (November 20 to January 3) and Dickens of a Christmas in Franklin (December 12 and 13). Glow Wild is the best fit for little kids because it opens at 5 pm and has an 85-foot tree and an ice rink.

What to do in Nashville for Christmas?

See ICE! featuring Home Alone at Gaylord Opryland, walk Cheekwood's Holiday LIGHTS, visit Glow Wild at the zoo, take a Christmas by Lamplight tour at the Hermitage, and shop the Nashvillage Holiday Market downtown. A day trip to Franklin for Dickens of a Christmas on December 12 and 13 is a Middle Tennessee tradition.

Where to see Santa in Nashville?

In 2026, Santa is at Gaylord Opryland (from November 6), Opry Mills and the Mall at Green Hills (from November 7), CoolSprings Galleria (from November 8), and Bass Pro Shops at Opry Mills, where the 4x6 photo is free. Nashville Yards offers free Santa photos on select weekends from November 28, and the Ryman hosts Santa on December 5.

What are some good indoor places to take pictures in Nashville?

Gaylord Opryland's decorated atriums are the strongest indoor holiday option, and the Parthenon in Centennial Park welcomes photography inside the museum except at one display case. The malls' Santa sets and the Ryman's December 5 Santa event are also indoors.

Is there an ice show in Nashville?

Yes. ICE! at Gaylord Opryland returns for 2026 with a Home Alone theme, carved from more than two million pounds of ice, and runs with A Country Christmas from November 6, 2026 to January 2, 2027.

What is the most Christmassy town in Tennessee near Nashville?

Franklin, in Williamson County just south of Nashville, is the easy pick. Its Main Street stays decorated all season, and the 41st Dickens of a Christmas fills downtown on December 12 and 13, 2026 with Victorian characters and carolers.

Can I hire a photographer at Cheekwood Holiday LIGHTS?

Not during the lights. Cheekwood's guidelines require booked portrait sessions to be off the grounds by 3:30 PM in November and December through the end of Holiday LIGHTS, with no after-hours sessions. Book a daytime session in the gardens and come back in the evening for candids.

Do photographers need a permit to shoot in Nashville parks?

Yes, for commercial work. Metro Parks charges $125 a day for Davidson County photographers and $150 for out-of-county photographers, or $250 and $300 for an annual permit, and requires insurance and at least 48 hours' notice.
