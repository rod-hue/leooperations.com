The Best Places to Propose in Nashville (and How to Pull It Off)

Twelve proven Nashville proposal spots with the light, crowds, parking and permit rules for each, plus how to hide a photographer and what to do if it rains.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated November 2026 · 12 min read

The best places to propose in Nashville are Love Circle for a sunset skyline view, the John Seigenthaler Pedestrian Bridge for downtown lights, the Allée steps at Percy Warner Park for a grand stone staircase, and Cheekwood for a garden setting with a ready-made proposal package. For something quieter, Radnor Lake and Belle Meade work beautifully, and for a night proposal, a Gulch rooftop like L.A. Jackson is hard to beat. Then check the permit rules, because several of Nashville's prettiest places charge photographers a fee or ban certain gear.

If you want the moment photographed without your partner spotting a camera, see our Nashville proposal photographer page for how a hidden-photographer plan works.

How to choose a Nashville proposal spot

Decide three things first: the light you want (sunset gold or after-dark city lights), how private it needs to be, and if you want professional photos, because that decides whether a permit is involved.

The skyline spots (Love Circle, the pedestrian bridge, the Gulch rooftops) are free or close to it but busy on weekend evenings. The gardens and estates (Cheekwood, Belle Meade, Arrington Vineyards) cost money and come with rules, but they feel private and the setting does half the work. The parks sit in between: free to visit, beautiful, and governed by Metro or state rules once a professional photographer shows up.

My opinion after years of photographing people in this city: the spot matters less than the timing within the spot. A weekday at 6 p.m. on the bridge beats a Saturday at 8 p.m. every time.

The 12 best places to propose in Nashville

1. Love Circle

Love Circle is the hilltop near Vanderbilt that locals drive up for the sunset, with a wide view of the downtown skyline. A historical marker listing places it on Love Circle in the 37212 zip code. It is an informal neighborhood overlook, not a staffed park, so there is no reservation system and parking is roadside along a narrow loop. Arrive 30 to 40 minutes before sunset on a weekday, keep the moment short, and respect the residents' driveways. Plan your photos for the last light, because the overlook gets dark fast once the sun is down.

2. John Seigenthaler Pedestrian Bridge

This 3,150 foot bridge opened in 1909 as the Sparkman Street Bridge and was renamed for John Seigenthaler in 2014. It gives you the full downtown skyline over the Cumberland River and has overlooks built into the railing, which make natural stopping points. Proposal photographers recommend golden hour for the skyline and blue hour for the lights, and avoiding peak weekend evenings. Frame it as a casual after-dinner walk from Broadway.

3. Percy Warner Park Allée steps

The stone steps at the Belle Meade Boulevard entrance to Percy Warner Park were built by the WPA in the 1930s and restored by Friends of Warner Parks. The long symmetrical staircase photographs like a much more expensive venue. The park is listed as open 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. Early morning is the quiet window; late afternoon brings walkers and runners.

4. Cheekwood Estate and Gardens

Cheekwood is the most turnkey option in the city. Its proposal packages include a Champagne Proposal Package (listed as a $600 value) with garden admission, a one-year dual membership, champagne flutes and a two-hour portrait photography session window, Tuesday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. The Holiday LIGHTS Proposal Package is $750, runs 5 to 9 p.m., and includes admission for four, with an optional $250 private heated igloo for 90 minutes. Both need at least 72 hours' notice. Cheekwood does not supply a photographer, so you bring your own. More on Cheekwood's photo rules below.

5. Radnor Lake State Natural Area

Radnor is the answer for the "nice, quiet-ish place to propose" question. The trails around the lake are calm, wooded and full of wildlife. The rules are strict: no going off trail, including to photograph or reach the shore, no weddings, no drones, no pets on the trails, and all vehicles out before dark. Propose on the trail at an overlook, not at the water's edge. Because it is a state natural area, a paid photographer falls under Tennessee State Parks commercial use rules (covered below).

6. Belle Meade Historic Site

Belle Meade offers a customizable proposal experience with wine, beer or an open bar and food from its culinary team, priced on request. The boxwood garden and grounds are the backdrop. Photographers need a Photography Pass reserved in advance, the grounds are open 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., and photography inside the mansion is prohibited.

7. Arrington Vineyards

Arrington, south of Nashville in Williamson County, allows proposals on the property but offers no proposal packages, and no vendors may set up or perform. Any photography of the proposal needs a $150 non-refundable photography permit, applied for at least 48 hours ahead, and flash and lighting equipment are not allowed. November and December hours are 11 a.m. to 8 p.m. Monday to Saturday and noon to 6 p.m. Sunday, so a late-afternoon proposal among the vines catches the sunset.

8. L.A. Jackson rooftop

For a rooftop proposal in Nashville, L.A. Jackson sits on the top floor of the Thompson Nashville at 401 11th Ave S in the Gulch. It is an indoor-outdoor bar with skyline views, which also makes it a decent rain backup. It gets loud on weekend nights when DJs play, so go early in the evening on a weeknight and call ahead about a quieter corner.

9. Centennial Park and the Parthenon

Centennial Park at 2500 West End Ave is open dawn to 11 p.m., and the full-scale Parthenon gives you columns and gold evening light. Inside, the Parthenon museum welcomes photography except at one display case, which makes it a strong indoor option. It is a Metro park, so a professional photographer needs the Metro Parks permit.

10. "What Lifts You" wings mural

Kelsey Montague painted the wings in the Gulch in 2016, 16 feet wide and 23 feet tall, and a line forms most weekends. It is a fun stop for the photos right after the yes. Go early on a weekday.

11. Dragon Park (Fannie Mae Dees Park)

The mosaic "Sea Serpent" by Pedro Silva, built with about 1,000 community volunteers and completed in 1980, is pure color and whimsy. It is a Metro park, so the permit rules apply.

12. Natchez Trace Parkway Double Arch Bridge

For a short drive out of town, the Double Arch Bridge at Milepost 438 stands 155 feet above Tennessee Highway 96 and runs 1,848 feet. The National Park Service points to the pull-off just north of the bridge for the best view of the bridge and hollow.

A note on the "I Believe in Nashville" mural: many older lists send couples there, but reports conflict about the current status of the original wall, so confirm it in person before you build a plan around it.

Nashville proposal photography permits and fees

This is where proposal plans go wrong.

  • Metro Nashville Parks (Centennial Park, Percy Warner, Shelby Bottoms, Dragon Park and every other Metro park): commercial photography needs a film, video and photography permit. Daily permits are $125 for Davidson County professionals and $150 for out-of-county, and annual permits are $250 and $300. A certificate of insurance and payment are required before the permit is issued, requests with under 48 hours' notice may not be granted, prop setups need a greenspace rental, and drones are not allowed outside designated flying areas.
  • Cheekwood: portrait sessions run $450 for members and $550 for non-members for two hours, with a $100 weekend fee and $100 an hour after hours. The photography guidelines ban props such as loose petals, balloons, candles and sparklers, ban speakers and live instruments while the gardens are open, bar sessions inside the mansion, and require November and December portrait guests to be off the grounds by 3:30 p.m. through the end of Holiday LIGHTS. If you book the LIGHTS proposal package, ask the Special Events Office exactly how a photographer can join you.
  • Arrington Vineyards: $150 permit, 48 hours ahead, no flash.
  • Belle Meade: Photography Pass reserved in advance; the fee is not published, so ask when you book.
  • Tennessee State Parks and natural areas (including Radnor Lake): commercial activity requires a Commercial Use Authorization from TDEC, and the state rule sets a market fee of 3% of gross receipts. Ask your photographer whether they hold one.

A professional proposal photographer should know which permit your spot needs before you ask.

How to plan a hidden-photographer proposal

The hidden photographer turns a proposal into a story you can show your grandchildren. Here is how a well-run one works.

  1. Pick the exact spot, not just the place. "Centennial Park" is not a plan. "The east steps of the Parthenon, facing the lake" is.
  2. Send the photographer photos of you both and describe what each of you will wear, so they can pick you out of a crowd.
  3. Agree on a signal. A hand on the back, a hat taken off, a specific line you will say. The photographer starts shooting on the signal.
  4. Choose the photographer's cover. A long lens from 30 to 50 yards away, posing as a tourist shooting the skyline, works on the bridge and at Love Circle. In gardens, a photographer "shooting flowers" disappears easily.
  5. Mark where you will kneel so you face the camera, not away from it. Turn your partner so the light falls on their face.
  6. Plan 20 to 30 minutes of portraits after the yes. This is when you get the relaxed, glowing photos.
  7. Have a backup for the reveal. If the spot is crowded, a pre-agreed second location 100 yards away saves the moment.

If you want help scouting or timing it, our proposal photographer page explains how we plan these.

Rain backups for a Nashville proposal

Middle Tennessee weather turns quickly, especially in spring. Build a backup into the plan from day one.

  • The Parthenon interior, open 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. most days, $15 for adults. Columns, the gallery and the statue hall give you drama indoors.
  • L.A. Jackson's indoor space, with skyline views through the glass.
  • Cheekwood's heated igloo add-on during Holiday LIGHTS, $250 for 90 minutes.
  • A booked hotel suite with a city view, decorated ahead of time. It is private, warm and fully under your control.
  • Clear umbrellas. Light rain can make beautiful photos in the right hands.

Check the forecast 72 hours out, which matches Cheekwood's booking window, and decide then.

Frequently asked questions

Where is a great place to propose marriage in Nashville?

Love Circle and the John Seigenthaler Pedestrian Bridge are the classic skyline choices, and Cheekwood is the easiest garden option because it sells proposal packages. For privacy, Radnor Lake and Belle Meade are calmer.

What are some romantic places to propose in Tennessee?

Near Nashville, the Natchez Trace Parkway Double Arch Bridge, Arrington Vineyards and Radnor Lake are favorites. In state parks, a hired photographer needs a Commercial Use Authorization from the state.

Where can I do a rooftop proposal in Nashville?

L.A. Jackson on top of the Thompson Nashville in the Gulch is the best known, with skyline views and an indoor space for bad weather. Go early on a weeknight, when it is quieter, and call ahead.

How much does it cost to hire a photographer for a proposal?

Local Lens lists Nashville proposal packages at $325 to $545 for one to two hours in 2026, and Joy puts engagement sessions at $300 to $800. Add any venue fees, such as Arrington's $150 permit or Cheekwood's $450 to $550 session fee. These are market ranges.

How far in advance do you need to book a proposal photographer?

Book two to four weeks ahead for a weekday and earlier for a weekend, the holidays or Valentine's Day. Remember the venue lead times too: Cheekwood needs at least 72 hours and Arrington and Metro Parks need at least 48 hours for permits.

Can I propose at Cheekwood?

Yes. Cheekwood sells a Champagne Proposal Package for weekday daytime visits and a $750 Holiday LIGHTS Proposal Package for evenings, both booked at least 72 hours ahead. You bring your own photographer, and props like petals and candles are not allowed.

What are the best places to propose in Nashville at night?

The pedestrian bridge at blue hour, a Gulch rooftop, and Cheekwood during Holiday LIGHTS (November 20, 2026 to January 3, 2027). Night proposals need a photographer who is comfortable working in low light without flash where flash is banned.
