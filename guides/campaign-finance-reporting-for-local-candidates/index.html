Campaign finance reporting for local and state candidates: treasurers, forms, deadlines and the fines that catch people

Who you file with, what goes in the report, when it is due and how to set up a system in week one, using the real rules from Texas, California, Florida, Georgia and Tennessee.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Founder, Leo Operations · Updated November 2026 · 11 min read

Local and state candidates report their money to a state ethics commission, a secretary of state or a local filing officer such as a city clerk or county election commission, not to the FEC. In Texas, Florida and Tennessee you must put a campaign treasurer on file before the first contribution comes in. Then you file reports on a fixed calendar that list donors above a set threshold (most commonly $100, according to NCSL), every expenditure and your cash on hand, with extra reports in the final weeks before Election Day. Missing a deadline costs real money. The late fine for most Texas reports is $500, and Florida fines climb to $500 a day.

The rules change at every state line, so this guide uses five states as worked examples. Confirm every date against your own state's current handbook.

Who do local candidates file campaign finance reports with?

Campaign finance for state and local races is state law. The FEC only handles federal races. Under federal law a person running for the U.S. House, Senate or President becomes a candidate after raising or spending more than $5,000 and must file a Statement of Candidacy within 15 days (FEC). Everyone else, from school board to state senate, files in the state system.

Where you file depends on the office:

  • Texas: city candidates file with the city clerk or secretary, school board candidates with the clerk or secretary of the school district, and county and precinct candidates with the county clerk or elections administrator (TEC candidate FAQ, TEC county filing guide). Statewide and legislative candidates file with the Texas Ethics Commission (TEC state filer guide).
  • Georgia: local candidates file with their local filing entity, which forwards the filings to the State Ethics Commission. State-level candidates file directly with the Commission (Georgia Ethics Commission).
  • Tennessee: candidates certify their political treasurer to the Registry of Election Finance for state races and to the county election commission for local races (Tennessee Registry of Election Finance).
  • California: local candidates file their Form 501 and Form 460 with the city clerk or county elections office, under statewide rules administered by the Fair Political Practices Commission (FPPC Campaign Manual 2, When and Where to File).

Call your filing officer the week you decide to run. The clerk who receives your reports knows the exact calendar for your election and the forms they accept.

Appoint a campaign treasurer before you raise or spend anything

This rule trips up new candidates because it applies before the campaign feels real. The treasurer paperwork comes ahead of the first donation and the first yard sign order.

  • Texas requires Form CTA when you become a candidate, even if you do not intend to accept contributions or make expenditures, and bars accepting contributions or making expenditures without it on file (TEC Form CTA instructions).
  • Florida bars a candidate from accepting contributions until Form DS-DE 9, which appoints the treasurer and designates the campaign depository, is filed with the filing officer (2026 Florida Candidate and Campaign Treasurer Handbook).
  • Tennessee requires the treasurer to be certified before any money is received or spent, other than incidental costs of deciding whether to run (Registry FAQ).
  • California requires the Form 501 candidate intention statement before soliciting or receiving contributions (FPPC Campaign Manual 2, Getting Started).

Some states let candidates serve as their own treasurer. Tennessee's appointment form says so directly (Form SS-1120). Pick someone else anyway. A candidate's time belongs on the phone and at the doors, and the best treasurers are available on deadline days and comfortable telling the candidate no.

What does a campaign treasurer do?

The job description is the same in every state, even though the forms differ.

  1. Files the appointment paperwork and opens the campaign bank account (in Florida, the designated depository).
  2. Records every contribution the day it arrives: name, address, date, amount and, where the state requires it, occupation and employer.
  3. Tracks each donor's running total, because itemization and contribution limits are usually based on aggregate giving, not single checks.
  4. Pays every campaign bill from the campaign account and keeps the invoice, receipt or contract behind each payment.
  5. Watches cash rules. Florida bars cash or cashier's check contributions over $50 per election from the same contributor. California will not let a committee accept $100 or more in cash, even if it hands back change (FPPC Campaign Activity FAQ).
  6. Files every report on time, including the short-turnaround reports near Election Day.
  7. Amends reports when an error turns up and keeps the records for as long as the state requires.

What goes into a state campaign finance report?

A standard report covers a defined period and shows six things:

  • Contributions received, itemized for donors above the state threshold and totaled for everyone below it.
  • In-kind contributions, meaning goods or services given instead of money: donated printing, food for a fundraiser, free office space. These are reported at their value even though no cash changed hands.
  • Loans, including money the candidate lends the campaign.
  • Expenditures, itemized by payee, date, amount and purpose.
  • Debts and obligations still unpaid at the end of the period.
  • Cash on hand, which must match the bank statement.

Aggregation catches people. The FPPC's own example: two $99 checks from one donor add up to $198, so that donor must be itemized. Texas adjusts its reporting thresholds every year for inflation, which is why its 2026 figure is $110 (TEC Form C/OH instructions).

Campaign finance reporting deadlines: the calendar that matters

Calendars have three layers: regular reports, pre-election reports and rapid reports for large late contributions.

CaliforniaSemiannual statementsPre-election statements on the FPPC schedule for your election (FPPC Campaign Manual 2)Form 497 within 24 hours for $1,000 or more from one source in the 90 days before the election (FPPC)

For the November 2026 general election, the Texas Ethics Commission's 30-day pre-election report was due Monday, October 5, 2026, and opposed candidates who had not chosen modified reporting had to file it even with no activity in the period (TEC 2026 notice).

The calendar does not end on Election Day either. Texas requires semiannual reports from all candidates, including those who lost, until they file a final report (TEC City Candidate Guide 2026).

Do small local campaigns have to file full reports?

Several states lighten the load for campaigns that stay small:

  • California: candidates who raise or spend $2,000 or more file a Form 410 Statement of Organization and the full Form 460; candidates under that line file the shorter Form 470 (FPPC toolkit, FPPC local candidate checklist).
  • Georgia: local candidates file Campaign Contribution Disclosure Reports once they exceed $2,500 (Georgia Ethics Commission).
  • Texas: opposed local candidates can choose modified reporting, but once contributions or expenditures exceed $1,140 (the 2026 figure), they lose that option and must file pre-election reports. Semiannual reports are still required of all candidates (TEC City Candidate Guide 2026, TEC Form ACTA instructions).

Plan as if you will cross the line. A contested race with mail and a paid canvassing program spends money fast, and full records from day one never need rebuilding under deadline.

What are the fines for late campaign finance reports?

Pre-election reports carry the steepest penalties because voters need them before they vote.

StateLate fineSource
Texas, most reports$500TEC
Texas, 8-day pre-election report$500 the first day plus $100 per day after, up to $10,000TEC 2026 notice
Florida, regular reports$50 per day for the first 3 days, then $500 per day, capped at 25% of receipts or expenditures for the period, whichever is greaterFlorida Division of Elections
Florida, report just before a primary or general$500 per day, same 25% capFlorida Division of Elections

Fines are the smaller risk. Your opponent reads your reports, and a late filing two weeks out becomes a mail piece.

How long do you have to keep campaign finance records?

Keep every bank statement, invoice and filed report for at least your state's retention period, and ask your filing officer for that period in writing at the start.

StateRetention rule
California4 years from the date the related campaign statement was filed (FPPC Campaign Manual)

Personal use, commingling and the other ways candidates get in trouble

California's FPPC training slides state the rules plainly: no personal use of campaign funds and no cash expenditures of $100 or more (FPPC training). Keep campaign money in the campaign account and your own money in yours.

The mistakes that show up most often in local races:

  • Paying a vendor from the candidate's personal card "just this once" and forgetting to record it.
  • Online donation pages that fail to collect occupation and employer, which leaves the treasurer chasing donors before a deadline.
  • A host covering fundraiser food or venue costs with no one recording the in-kind contribution. Our guide to hosting a political fundraiser covers how to log host costs.
  • Skipping the zero-activity report.
  • Missing a 24-hour or two-business-day report because a large check arrived on a Friday.
  • Treating the campaign as finished on election night while semiannual reports keep coming due.

How to set up campaign finance reporting in your first week

Build the system before the first fundraising call.

  1. Call your filing officer, get the full reporting calendar for your election and put every due date on a shared calendar with an internal deadline five days earlier.
  2. File the treasurer appointment and any depository designation, then open a dedicated campaign bank account.
  3. Set up the donation page on your campaign website to collect every field your state requires, from the first dollar.
  4. Keep a contribution log that records each deposit the day it lands, with a running total per donor.
  5. Pay every vendor from the campaign account and attach the invoice to the transaction. Field, printing and media vendors should invoice in a form your treasurer can report from.
  6. Reconcile the log to the bank statement every month, not the week a report is due.
  7. Build reporting dates into your campaign budget so big buys and big asks are timed with the calendar in view.

Frequently asked questions

What does a campaign treasurer do?

A campaign treasurer opens and runs the campaign bank account, records every contribution and expenditure, tracks each donor's running total and files the campaign finance reports on time. The treasurer also handles amendments and keeps the records for the period the state requires. In Texas, Florida and Tennessee the treasurer must be on file before the campaign takes in money.

Do local candidates file campaign finance reports with the FEC?

No. The FEC covers only candidates for the U.S. House, Senate and President, who must register after raising or spending more than $5,000 (FEC). City, county, school board, judicial and state legislative candidates file with their state ethics agency, secretary of state or a local filing officer such as a city clerk or county election commission.

Do I have to file a campaign finance report if I did not raise any money?

Often, yes. Texas required opposed candidates who had not chosen modified reporting to file the 30-day pre-election report for the November 2026 election even with no activity (TEC). Other states offer a short form below a threshold, such as California's Form 470 for campaigns under $2,000. Ask your filing officer which applies to you.

Can a candidate be their own campaign treasurer?

Some states allow it. Tennessee's treasurer appointment form states that a candidate may appoint himself or herself (Form SS-1120). A separate treasurer is the stronger setup, because it frees the candidate to campaign and puts a second person on every transaction.

How much is the fine for a late campaign finance report?

It depends on the state and the report. In Texas most late reports cost $500, and a late 8-day pre-election report adds $100 a day after the first day, up to $10,000. Florida starts at $50 a day and rises to $500 a day after three days.

What is a 24-hour or two-business-day report?

It is a rapid report for large contributions that arrive close to the election. In California and Georgia the trigger is $1,000 or more.

Where can I find state campaign finance reports?

Reports are public records filed with your state ethics commission, secretary of state or local filing officer, and many states post them online. Local candidate reports may sit with the city or county clerk. Reading your opponent's past reports is standard opposition research.
