When to Book a Wedding Photographer and Videographer, and What Happens After You Do

Booking windows, deposits, how many photos to expect, delivery timelines, tipping and the weeks after the wedding, explained for couples and the families helping pay.

By Rodriquez Wright II, Leo Operations · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

Book your wedding photographer and videographer 9 to 12 months before the wedding, and 12 to 18 months ahead if you are marrying on a Saturday in September or October or in a busy city. Lock the venue and date first, then book photo and video next, before most other vendors. The average engagement in the US lasts about 15 months according to The Knot's 2025 Real Weddings Study, so most couples have time, but the best people for peak dates go early.

The rest of this guide covers what you are actually signing up for: deposits, how many photos you will get, when they arrive, how they should be backed up, whether to tip, and what the weeks after the wedding look like.

How far in advance should I book a wedding photographer?

Photographers and videographers can only be in one place on a given Saturday. That is the whole problem. A florist can do three weddings in a day. A photographer can do one. So the scarcity is per date, and it is sharpest on the dates everyone wants.

SituationBook this far ahead
Saturday in September or October, major metro12 to 18 months
Saturday in spring or early summer10 to 14 months
Friday or Sunday, any season6 to 12 months
Weekday or winter wedding4 to 9 months
Destination wedding (photographer traveling)12 to 18 months
Elopement or courthouse weddingA few weeks to a few months

These are practical market ranges from how booking calendars fill, not a published standard. Vendor surveys and photographer blogs cluster around 9 to 15 months, with photographers commonly reporting that most couples book in that window.

Two rules matter more than the exact number. First, do not book anyone until the venue contract is signed and the date is final. A photographer cannot hold a date you might change. Second, if you love one specific photographer, contact them the week you get your venue. Waiting "until after the holidays" is how couples lose their first choice.

Is it the same for videographers?

Yes, with one difference. Fewer couples hire video (The Knot reports roughly 37% do), so there are fewer good videographers in most markets. Book them at the same time as your photographer, ideally from the same team so the two crews work together instead of competing for angles. More on that in our guide to booking a wedding photographer and videographer together.

When is peak wedding season?

Fall. October has been the most popular US wedding month for years, accounting for about 17% of 2024 weddings, with September close behind at about 15%, according to The Knot data reported by Spectrum News. Roughly four in ten weddings now happen between September and November. June, May and August follow.

What that means for you:

  • October Saturdays go first, often a year and a half ahead.
  • Holiday weekends (Memorial Day, Labor Day) are busy and sometimes priced higher.
  • January through March is the true off-season in most of the country. You will have more choice and sometimes better pricing.
  • In hot Southern markets, including Nashville, midsummer can be slower than spring and fall because outdoor ceremonies in July heat are rough on everyone.

Deposits and retainers: what to expect

Almost every professional photographer and videographer requires a signed contract and a retainer to hold your date. Industry norms (market ranges, not a rule):

  • Retainer size: commonly 25% to 50% of the total, sometimes a flat amount.
  • Refundability: retainers are usually nonrefundable, because the studio turned down other couples for your date.
  • Balance due: typically 30 days to 2 weeks before the wedding, sometimes on the day.
  • Payment plans: many studios split the balance into monthly payments. Ask.

Read the contract for these clauses before paying anything:

  1. What happens if you postpone. Can the retainer move to a new date?
  2. What happens if the photographer is sick or injured. Who replaces them, and do you approve the replacement?
  3. Delivery deadlines for photos and films, in writing.
  4. Who owns the images and what you can do with them (printing and sharing should always be allowed).
  5. Overtime rate per hour.
  6. Travel fees if your venue is outside their area.

If the parents are paying, put the couple's names on the contract anyway, or both. The couple is the one making decisions on the day.

How many photos does a wedding photographer deliver?

A common rule of thumb is 50 to 75 edited photos per hour of coverage, a figure Narrative uses as a typical professional range. Using that range as a baseline:

CoverageTypical edited photos delivered
4 hours200 to 400
6 hours300 to 500
8 hours400 to 800
10 hours500 to 900

A photographer may take 2,000 to 3,500 frames on a full day and narrow them down. More is not better. A tight gallery of 500 strong, well-edited photos beats 1,500 near-duplicates you will never scroll through. What you should check is whether the gallery includes the people who matter: every grandparent, every sibling, the family groupings your culture or faith expects. That is a planning job, not a numbers job, and our wedding shot list shows how to write it down.

How long does it take to get wedding photos back?

Delivery times vary widely across the industry. These are typical market ranges:

DeliverableCommon industry turnaround
Sneak peek (10 to 30 photos)24 hours to 2 weeks
Full edited gallery4 to 12 weeks
Highlight film1 to 4 months
Feature film or full ceremony2 to 6 months
Printed album1 to 3 months after you approve the design

Peak season stretches all of these. A studio that shoots every October Saturday is editing into January. The fix is simple: ask for a written delivery date in the contract, and ask when their last three couples received their galleries.

Galleries, backups and albums

A professional should protect your photos at three points:

  • On the day: cameras that record to two memory cards at once, so one card failure does not lose the ceremony.
  • After the wedding: files copied to at least two separate drives plus an off-site or cloud backup before cards are reformatted.
  • After delivery: an online gallery you can download from, usually hosted for a set period (often 6 months to a few years). Download everything to your own computer and a cloud account the week it arrives. Do not rely on the photographer's gallery as your archive.

Albums are worth it more often than couples expect. Digital files get scattered across phones and old laptops. A well-made album sits on a shelf for 40 years and gets pulled out at anniversaries. Parents often order their own smaller copies. Ask what albums cost upfront so it does not surprise you later.

How much should you tip a wedding photographer?

Tipping is optional and appreciated, not required. The Knot's guidance suggests 5% to 15% of the contract or $50 to $200 per team member for photographers and videographers.

  • Who: Associate photographers, second shooters, videographers and assistants. Studio owners generally are not expected to receive a tip, per The Knot, though many couples still give one when service was exceptional.
  • How much: $50 to $200 per person is the most common approach for crews.
  • When: At the end of the night, in a labeled envelope, usually handed off by a parent, the planner or the best man so the couple does not have to think about it.
  • Check the contract first: If a service charge or gratuity is already built in, you do not need to add one.

The tip photographers and videographers value most costs nothing: a detailed online review, and tagging them when you post. That is how small studios get their next couple.

What happens after the wedding?

Here is the normal sequence once the last guest leaves.

  1. Night of or next day: files are backed up in multiple places.
  2. First week or two: a sneak peek of a few favorite photos, sometimes a short social clip.
  3. Weeks 2 to 12: culling, color correction and editing of the full gallery. Video editing runs in parallel and usually takes longer.
  4. Gallery delivery: a private online link. Download everything immediately.
  5. Film delivery: highlight film first, longer edits later.
  6. Album design: you choose favorites, the studio designs, you approve, it prints.
  7. Thank-yous: many couples use gallery photos for thank-you cards, so ask for a few early if you want to send cards quickly.

Booking engagement photos? That usually happens 6 to 9 months before the wedding. See our engagement photos guide.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should I book a wedding photographer?

Most couples should book 9 to 12 months ahead. For a Saturday in September or October, or a popular venue in a large city, aim for 12 to 18 months. Book only after your venue and date are confirmed.

How far in advance do wedding photographers book up?

Popular photographers often have their peak fall Saturdays filled a year or more in advance. Weekday, Sunday and winter dates stay open much longer. If you have a specific photographer in mind, reach out as soon as your venue is signed.

How many hours should you book a wedding photographer?

Six to eight hours covers most weddings from getting ready through the main reception moments. Shorter weddings and elopements can work with 2 to 4 hours. Build the hours from your actual timeline (see our wedding photography timeline), not from a package name.

How many photos does a wedding photographer deliver?

A typical range is 50 to 75 edited photos per hour of coverage, so roughly 400 to 600 for an 8 hour day. Totals vary by photographer and by how much happens. Ask to see two or three full galleries before booking.

Is $200 a good tip for a wedding photographer?

Yes. $200 sits at the top of The Knot's suggested $50 to $200 per team member range. If two photographers worked your day, many couples give $100 to $200 each. Tipping remains optional.

How long does it take to get wedding photos back?

Industry norms run from 4 to 12 weeks for a full gallery, often with a sneak peek in the first week or two. Videos usually take longer. Make sure the delivery deadline is written into your contract.

Should I pay the photographer deposit or should my parents?

Whoever is funding the photography can pay, but the contract should name the couple so decisions on the day are theirs. Many families split it: parents cover the retainer as a gift, the couple handles the rest.
